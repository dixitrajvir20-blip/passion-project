# Interactive tools beyond the five calculators: the research (22 September 2026)

Rajvir asked for more standalone interactive tools "like the budget calculator, to help people", after JP Morgan Chase and other finance sites. A workflow (`workflows/scripts/interactive-tools-research-*.js`, run wf_c44291a9-308) did the research: four sweeps that opened the tools on US bank hubs, nonprofit and regulator sites, Indian sites and European sites (126 tools catalogued), plus a read of this repo's own lessons and kit; three designers proposing a set from different angles; two judges (content and legal; engineering and teaching); one synthesis; then two skeptics per chosen tool (facts; safety and scope). The machine-readable result, with every skeptic's problems and fixes attached, is `interactive-tools-spec.json` beside this file. The build works from a revision of that spec that applies every fix.

## In the owner's words

Three proposals were researched from the free tools at Chase, Bank of America, NerdWallet, Bankrate, the CFPB, NGPF and Indian and European public sites, and two reviewers checked them against the site's rules and its code. Nine tools are to be built now. That takes India to 12 tools and Europe and the United States to 11 each, and every tool opens on the numbers from a lesson that already teaches it. Take-home pay turns a payslip into what reaches your account, line by line. Buffer target says on which payday a buffer for your must-pay costs is ready. Card minimum shows how long a card takes to clear at the minimum payment and what that costs. Yearly rate (India only) turns a fee or an instalment plan into a yearly rate. Pay-later plans (Europe and the US) shows what one payday has left after the instalments, and rent share (Europe) sets rent and bills against net pay and Eurostat's 40% line, with the cost of moving in. Tax on side income works out US self-employment tax and an example income tax set-aside, and in India what comes back from tax withheld on fees; it never works out income tax slabs. Two drills of made-up screens are added: 'spot the fake' gets a Europe edition built on the name check banks now run before a transfer, and a new job-offer drill runs in all three editions, each with ordinary cases so refusing everything is not the answer. Pricing a job from its hours and a money-tips drill are held for a later batch, and ideas such as a 'surprise cost' simulator, a credit-score sandbox, tax-regime and trading calculators and any 'which is better' tool were turned down because they rank choices, invent numbers or read as advice. The tools page will be grouped by life moment, a copied link will carry numbers after a # so they never reach a server, and several lessons will point to their new tools (the se-tax lesson summary also needs one wording fix); nothing will be pushed or deployed without you.

## The nine chosen

- **Take-home pay, line by line** (`take-home-pay`, calculator, in, eu, us): How much of my pay actually reaches my account? Modelled on: FDIC Money Smart's lettered pay statement (every line defined, works without JS), Wikifin's fictional student payslip, Jupiter's India line order (basic, PF, professional tax, TDS, net), and proposal . Lessons: in/money-basics/first-payslip, eu/money-basics/first-payslip, us/money-basics/first-paycheck. Skeptics raised 29 points, all to be applied before it ships.
- **A loan's cost as a yearly rate** (`yearly-rate`, calculator, in): I get this much and pay back that much: what is that as a yearly rate? Modelled on: Axis Finance's APR calculator, where a fee taken at the start raises the yearly cost, and the Key Facts Statement itself; Groww's flat-vs-reducing inputs. What is different: terms in days, so a 7-day . Lessons: in/protect-your-money/cost-of-borrowing, in/start-something/first-business-loan. Skeptics raised 28 points, all to be applied before it ships.
- **Card balance at the minimum payment** (`card-minimum`, calculator, in, us): If I pay only the minimum, how long does my card take to clear, and what does it cost? Modelled on: Bankrate's minimum payment calculator (the rule as an input, the minimum against a fixed payment, the schedule behind a fold); Khan Academy's statement warning table; proposal 1's month-one statement . Lessons: us/money-basics/credit-file, in/protect-your-money/cost-of-borrowing. Skeptics raised 23 points, all to be applied before it ships.
- **Buffer target and the payday it is reached** (`buffer-target`, calculator, in, eu, us): On which payday will a buffer for my must-pay costs be ready? Modelled on: NerdWallet's emergency fund calculator (essentials × months), crossed with Chase's time-to-goal tool (rate 0% by default) and Practical Money Skills' 'already enough' state. What is different: months . Lessons: in/money-basics/one-month-buffer, eu/money-basics/three-month-buffer, us/money-basics/first-cushion. Skeptics raised 30 points, all to be applied before it ships.
- **Pay-later plans against one payday** (`pay-later-payday`, calculator, eu, us): What is left of this payday once my pay-later plans take their share? Modelled on: The two lessons' deduction explorers and BudgetPlanner's add and remove rows; NerdWallet's small capped 'add another' rows. What is different: no provider names and no mascot, and 0% is stated as 0% w. Lessons: eu/credit-and-fraud/bnpl-is-credit, us/how-business-works/bnpl. Skeptics raised 32 points, all to be applied before it ships.
- **Payments: spot the fake** (`spot-the-fake`, drill, in, eu): Would I catch a payment scam before I tapped? Modelled on: SEC HoweyTrade (a realistic fake, then a debrief naming each flag); the Betaalvereniging IBAN-name check outcomes; Bank of America's quiz (a one-line why, neutral feedback). What is different: HTML-fi. Lessons: in/protect-your-money/upi-fraud-and-the-clock, eu/credit-and-fraud/instant-transfer-check. Skeptics raised 36 points, all to be applied before it ships.
- **Job offers: spot the fake** (`job-offer-check`, drill, in, eu, us): Would I spot a fake job offer before I replied? Modelled on: The Europol and EBF #DontBeAMule warning signs as the rubric; the FTC's job-scam, fake-check and task-scam pages; SEC HoweyTrade's fake followed by a debrief. What is different: no name entry, no rewa. Lessons: in/protect-your-money/money-mule, eu/credit-and-fraud/money-mule, us/how-business-works/job-scam. Skeptics raised 31 points, all to be applied before it ships.
- **Rent and bills as a share of net pay** (`rent-share`, calculator, eu): How much of my net pay would this room take? Modelled on: The French government's student budget simulator (one-off moving costs counted separately, reference figures with a named source) and Nibud's running monthly total. What is different: a few numbers an. Lessons: eu/money-basics/moving-out. Skeptics raised 33 points, all to be applied before it ships.
- **Tax on side income and fees** (`side-income-tax`, calculator, in, us): How much tax is still to pay, or to come back, on what my work brought in? Modelled on: The se-tax lesson's split and the tds-refund lesson's deduction ledger; CalcWise's eligibility gate with its reason; the IRS estimated taxes page for the $1,000 line and the four dates. What is differ. Lessons: us/start-something/se-tax, in/money-basics/tds-refund. Skeptics raised 32 points, all to be applied before it ships.

## Held for later

- `price-from-hours`: Both judges ranked it 9th or 10th. It is side-hustle profit run backwards (hours × rate + the job's own costs = price; pins ₹14,600 and $225), so build it as a 'from hours to a price' mode on the side-hustle page in the next batch, as the engineering judge suggested, then move pricing-a-service and pricing-a-job to it. Until then those lessons keep side-hustle.
- `money-tip-check`: The content-legal judge would build it only under three conditions: return claims appear solely on screens marked as made-up fakes, the market-signal test exemption is scoped to those lines, and no security, index or price appears anywhere. The engineering judge rejected it for weakening the SEBI guardrail. Because both judges did not rank it, it waits. Build it on the drill bank once the content-reviewer approves a scoped exemption inside tests/unit/drills.test.ts, never in lessons.test.ts.
- `side-income-tax (Europe)`: The EU lesson states there is no single EU figure for small earnings. Add Europe when national presets with sourced, dated rates exist, or when an EU lesson teaches a set-aside share.
- `yearly-rate (Europe, US)`: The APRC convention (effective, Directive 2023/2225 Annex I) and Reg Z's must be verified, and a lesson in each edition must teach them first (the engineering judge's condition).
- `card-minimum (Europe)`: No EU lesson teaches revolving card balances, and minimum rules differ by country.
- `rent-share (India, US)`: India has no official line, and the US line (HUD's 30%) is measured on gross income. Add each with a lesson.
- `savings-goal-mode`: Time to any goal, or the monthly amount needed to reach one by a date (Chase, Bank of America, MoneyHelper), is buffer-target with the target typed in. Add it as a second mode of that tool, not a new page.
- `savings-in-todays-prices`: Buying power after inflation belongs as an 'in today's prices' row on the savings tool. Only eu/compound-and-inflation teaches it, and an India default needs a dated inflation source.
- `loan-what-ifs`: Fixed payment to months, and the effect of one extra payment, extend the loan tool. Add them as 'change one thing' controls there.
- `drill-cue-question-and-tally`: Proposal 1's second question per screen ('Which line gives it away?') and an end-of-set tally need a Drill.astro schema change and a CSP-hashed inline script. Add them after the banks have settled.
- `us-payments-drill`: A US payment-app scam set (the FTC reports a $380 median loss on payment-app scams) waits for a US lesson. Then add 'us' to spot-the-fake's regions with a bank.
- `eu-payslip-presets`: Country presets for take-home pay, one country at a time, each with sourced, dated rates, a reviewer and tests. The generic typed-amount version ships first.
- `withholding-year-so-far`: A 'year so far' row on take-home pay (India TDS against tax due; a US W-2 against the return), once the tax-due figure can be linked to the official calculators.
- `advance-tax-block`: India advance-tax instalments as a final block of side-income-tax, once an India lesson teaches advance tax. Most readers fall under the ₹10,000 threshold.
- `student-loan-in-school-interest`: US only. It needs dated per-year rates, verification of the capitalisation rule after 1 July 2026, and a lesson. The monthly-payment question is already answered by the loan tool.
- `insurance-claim-who-pays`: The arithmetic is sound (NGPF Bummer), but no lesson teaches deductibles or co-pays yet.
- `euro-transfer-cost`: The cost of sending money out of the euro area (Banca d'Italia's remittance calculator) is a strong EU fit. Build it with an Erasmus or family-abroad lesson.
- `club-printables`: Printable worksheets of each tool's example for Business Lab clubs (PROJECT_BRIEF v2 teacher kit), after the tools settle.

## Turned down

- `surprise-cost`: Both judges rejected it. It sets borrowing routes side by side for a minor ('a lending app … the card … wait for payday'). Even unranked they read as 'which is better', and the ₹0 'wait' row is an implicit winner. It puts a lending app in front of the reader as a way to cover a gap, runs close to the SPENT backfire LEARNING_DESIGN warns about, is medium-large to build, and depends on another tool. buffer-target answers the buffer question with one ceil().
- `pay-later-paydays`: Proposal 1's simulation scripts an 'add one more purchase' step to end in a shortfall and a fee: a consequence-of-spending design that can moralise. It needs day windows, predict-then-open steps and a static fallback. pay-later-payday teaches the same sum on the reader's own plans at about a third of the build.
- `minimum-payment`: Superseded by card-minimum. Its unverified 18% GST default changes the lesson's ₹19,700 to ₹19,826, its US defaults ($1,500, at least $35) are not the lesson's $400 and $25, and three columns are cramped at 360px. Its month-one statement ledger was kept, inside card-minimum.
- `loan-yearly-rate`: Its India default (₹10,000 at 2% a month) is not the lesson's. The India headline convention was still 'verify against its annex'. Its US payday example has no US lesson behind it, and it needs three legal conventions plus a flat/reducing/typed mode switch. The India-only yearly-rate covers the lesson that exists.
- `payslip-decoder`: It duplicates take-home-pay with about 18 inputs whose fields switch between a rule and a typed amount (poor fit for useFields). It also prints the unverified EPS split '₹1,458 to the pension scheme, ₹642 to your EPF' as fact; at a ₹15,000 pension wage ceiling the split would be ₹1,250 and ₹850. Its 12-month staleness test was kept.
- `take-home-pay (proposal 2's version)`: It states a tax conclusion ('salary up to ₹12,75,000 a year usually carries none', with the standard deduction 'to confirm') and carries pay in query-string links, which send the figure to the host. Proposal 0's version, with typed TDS and # links, is built instead, with proposal 2's Europe implied-rate lines.
- `rent-share-of-pay`: Proposal 2's three-edition version invents a use for India, which has no official line, and needs a second gross-pay input for HUD's 30% in the US, where no lesson teaches it. The Europe-only rent-share is built.
- `credit-score-sandbox`: Scoring models are proprietary, so any point values would be invented (NGPF Credit Clash's +15/−30 are game numbers). It would teach wrong figures.
- `debt-ordering`: Snowball against avalanche answers 'which order', which is advice-shaped (Chase's quiz recommends a method), and few 15–21-year-olds hold several debts.
- `income-tax-regime-calculator`: Slab logic goes stale (the department's own FAQ still showed AY 2024-25 figures), and choosing a regime is advice. Link the official calculator instead.
- `trading-cost-calculators`: Brokerage and F&O margin tools are trading aids for a documented harm: under-30s were 43% of F&O traders in FY26, and 89% of them lost money (SEBI).
- `decision-tools`: 'Should I buy it?', 'Pay down debt, save or invest?' and 'Do I have too much debt?' (Bank of America, Practical Money Skills) are named for the reader's decision and output a recommendation, which the education-not-advice rule bars.
- `personal-action-plans`: 'What to tackle first' plans (MoneyHelper Money Navigator style) recommend what to do with money.
- `net-worth-and-insurance-need`: SEBI's net-worth tool has 40 rows for readers who own almost nothing, and an insurance-need tool outputs a cover amount to buy.
- `financial-health-score`: Scoring minors' financial well-being is sensitive, its norms are adult, and its feedback (fund sizes, cover amounts) is advice.
- `role-reversal-games`: Shady Sam-style games reward manipulation, ask for a name, ship heavy bundles and load third-party analytics. Only their pressure-line cues carry over, into job-offer-check's reveals.
- `spending-cut-trackers`: 'Three cuts, one year' and lunch trackers (Chase, Practical Money Skills, Finanzas para todos) moralise through their defaults (eating out, smoking), and no lesson teaches them.
- `fees-vs-returns`: 'Impact of 1%' and cost-of-delay tools need an expected-return assumption next to investing, where the SEBI education rule and the no-returns rule make every default risky.
- `flat-vs-reducing-standalone`: A 'save ₹X' comparison gives a verdict. yearly-rate already covers flat offers: type the printed instalment.
- `credit-utilisation`: A single division that invites gaming a score against a '30%' guideline that needs careful sourcing. If anything, it belongs as an explorable in the credit-file lesson.
- `personal-inflation-rate`: It needs dated HICP weight tables for each country and 12 spending categories, which is too large for a small tool, and no lesson teaches it.
- `gst-vat-share`: Small value for readers far below the registration thresholds. It fits as a line in the pricing-abroad lesson.
- `hra-gratuity-epf-corpus`: The HRA exemption applies only under the old regime, gratuity is one line of take-home-pay's hint, and an EPF corpus is a 40-year projection. None of them is a first earner's question.

## What the sweeps found worth copying, and not


### India: what an 18-year-old with a first salary, a UPI app and a loan app would search for ("ctc to in hand calculator", 

- Pattern: Three inputs and a live answer: loan, rate, time with sliders or boxes. EMI, FD, SIP, RD and flat-vs-reducing all use this shape (Zerodha, Groww). Business Lab's tool kit already matches it.
- Pattern: Opening on a filled example: Zerodha opens on a worked ₹10 lakh / 6% / 5-year case, while SEBI's tools open blank and KreditBee shows '₹ NaN'. The filled state is what makes a tool usable on first sight.
- Pattern: Two methods on the same inputs, side by side: flat vs reducing (Groww, KreditBee), old vs new regime (Groww, Income Tax Dept, 1 Finance), presumptive vs actual profit (CalcWise). This fits Business Lab as two ledgers with no verdict line.
- Pattern: A deduction chain as rows, monthly and yearly: Jupiter's basic → HRA → allowances → PF → professional tax → net before tax → TDS → net after tax is the clearest CTC-to-in-hand layout. A monthly/yearly toggle beside it helps.
- Pattern: Assumptions printed next to the answer: basic as 40–50% of CTC, PF at 12% of basic, professional tax varies by state (ClearTax, Jupiter). Stating the assumption is what makes an estimate honest.
- Pattern: Same money, three views: nominal, after tax, after inflation (Zerodha FD). The strongest teaching device found for savings.
- Pattern: Delay tables: the monthly amount needed if you start 1–10 years later (SEBI cost of delay). One table row per 'what if' teaches without a chart.
- Pattern: Lettered ledgers: (A) + (B) − (C) = answer (SEBI young earners). The lettering lets the result sentence refer back to lines.
- Do not copy: Product funnels attached to tax and salary tools: Groww puts 'SAVE TAX WITH ELSS FUNDS' or a mutual-fund account CTA on salary, HRA, gratuity, GST, TDS, EPF, RD and PPF calculators, so a tax question ends in a fund pitch. Business Lab: no product categories framed as the answer to a calculation, and no 'save tax by investing' lines.
- Do not copy: Account sign-up with a phone number field on calculator pages (Zerodha, 'New to Zerodha? Sign-up for free'). Business Lab: no fields beyond the calculation, nothing typed leaves the browser.
- Do not copy: Loan CTAs placed after the result: 'Apply Now' after results, app-store badges and 'instant' loan cards (KreditBee); 'Check eligibility' for loans against mutual funds (Groww EMI and flat-vs-reducing); 'Apply Now' in the header (Axis Finance). The moment a reader sees the cost is exactly when these sites offer the loan.
- Do not copy: Lead generation on tax tools: 'File ITR Now' sticky button and 'Hire a CA' (ClearTax); 'Book a free consultation … Your first financial plan is free' (1 Finance); paid filing tiers and WhatsApp/phone contact (EZTax).
- Do not copy: Dubious services next to debt tools: Swipe2Cash advertises card-to-bank cash transfers and cash bill payment beside its minimum-due calculator. This is borrowing at card rates to pay card debt.
- Do not copy: Named banks and apps in inputs: a bank dropdown (Swipe2Cash) and bank-specific minimum-due tables. Business Lab: the rule is an input with a labelled example, never a bank name.

### Nonprofit, university and regulator interactives: NGPF (Arcade + Interactives), SEC investor.gov, CFPB, IRS, FDIC Money 

- Pattern: Predict, then reveal, then difference, then reflect (NGPF Time Machine): the reader commits to a guess (drag or keyboard, Enter to lock), then sees guess, answer and gap, then gets one reflection question. A status message tells screen readers the result appeared.
- Pattern: Result sentences built from templates with the computed figures (Practical Money Skills), including edge-case sentences: 'you already have enough' and 'this payment can't clear the debt'.
- Pattern: Working shown as labelled lines (PMS Emergency Fund: expenses x months; needed / months). This is Business Lab's ledger by another name.
- Pattern: Solve either way: enter the payment to get months, or the months to get the payment (PMS credit card payoff). Forward and reverse versions of the same maths (investor.gov compound growth vs savings goal).
- Pattern: Uncertainty shown as a band: investor.gov plots the same savings at the rate and at plus or minus a chosen variance, rather than one promised figure.
- Pattern: Enter the amount as you actually pay it, then convert (FDIC expense log 'how often' select). Removes the mental maths that breaks budgets for irregular costs.
- Pattern: Documents taught in place: lettered markers A to H with a definition table that works without JS and prints (FDIC). Tap a line to highlight it plus a 'check this' question (CFPB Loan Estimate). Folded table sections with find-the-figure questions (Khan statement).
- Pattern: Same loan, several offers (Shady Sam): comparing monthly payment against total interest across terms makes the trade-off visible without saying which to pick.
- Do not copy: Third-party trackers on nonprofit game sites: shadysam.com loads Google Analytics and Google Tag Manager (a page_view hit to google-analytics.com was observed). Practical Money Skills loads Google Tag Manager, gstatic charts and a third-party cookie panel. Payback defines Facebook SDK functions for sharing. Business Lab must ship none of these; charts are drawn locally.
- Do not copy: Server-side calculation: investor.gov posts the calculator form to its server (Drupal form_build_id; results returned in drupalSettings), so the typed figures leave the browser. Business Lab calculates on the device only.
- Do not copy: Personal details asked before play: Shady Sam and Slick Wheels ask for a first name; Payback asks GPA band, extracurriculars and home state; JA Finance Park needs a login; FDIC suggests an account for certificates. Business Lab opens on an edition example and asks nothing personal.
- Do not copy: Tool names framed as the reader's decision: 'How Much Car Can You Afford?', 'Do I Have Too Much Debt?', 'Do I Have Enough Retirement Money?', 'How Much House Can You Afford?'. Business Lab names describe the calculation ('Share of take-home going to repayments').
- Do not copy: Commands and verdicts in results: PMS says you must increase your payment; PMS says housing and consumer debt 'should not' exceed 28% and 30% and that more could hurt your score; CFPB tells you to consider whether it is more debt than you can afford. Business Lab states the arithmetic and leaves the choice alone; any threshold is cited and labelled a guideline.
- Do not copy: Rates shown as outcomes without an example label: NGPF Time Machine says 'imagine... it earns a 10% return every year' and uses '7% return every year' with no 'example, not a promise' tag. Business Lab labels every illustrative rate.

### Europe: euro area first, then the UK as an English reference. Opened on 22 Sept 2026 with WebFetch and a JS-capable brow

- Pattern: Name the tool with the reader's question and route by question: Wikifin's credit tool opens on three questions ('How much do I repay each month?', 'How much can I borrow?', 'How long will I be repaying?'), each its own URL; the AMF titles its fees tool with the question itself.
- Pattern: Fill-in-the-sentence inputs ('I want to borrow [ ] at [ ]% over [ ] months'): compact on a phone and it reads as the question. It needs real labels on each blank.
- Pattern: A running total that is always visible (Nibud's 'your spending per month' on each of 27 steps), so the answer grows while you type.
- Pattern: A frequency on every budget line (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, yearly) converted to a monthly figure (La finance pour tous youth budget, Finanzas para todos, Banca d'Italia): teenagers' money arrives weekly or as one-off gifts.
- Pattern: Permission to skip and to estimate ('if you don't know yet, estimate it'), so an unknown field doesn't stop the reader.
- Pattern: Reference averages with a named, dated source beside the field (Nibud Student Survey 2024; OVE Repères 2023 food range on the French student simulator): a benchmark, never a target.
- Pattern: A drawn, fictional document with a 'show definitions' switch and a 'these amounts are made up' footnote (Wikifin student payslip). It matches Business Lab's Document + deduction chain.
- Pattern: Making a hidden cost a visible ledger line: the rate typed versus the TAEG (Banca d'Italia, Banco de Portugal), the exchange-rate margin versus the mid rate (Banca d'Italia remittances), fees versus the gross return (AMF).
- Do not copy: Tracking on tool pages: the regulator-run Finanzas para todos loads the Facebook/Meta pixel (connect.facebook.net fbevents.js) and preconnects to Google Analytics on pages where people type their income and debts. Nibud's student tool loads Google Tag Manager while saying it stores no IP address or cookies. Business Lab: no third-party scripts on any tool.
- Do not copy: Third-party code and fonts: Finanztip's widget pulls Inter from a CDN inside a shadow DOM with Tailwind, and Finanzas para todos loads a Font Awesome kit. Business Lab stays self-hosted, and Inter is on its banned list.
- Do not copy: Nudging toward products: Finanztip will not calculate net pay until you pick a real, named health insurer with its rate, promotes 'see if you can save at your insurer', and is paid through starred affiliate links. Verbraucherzentrale and the Dutch BNPL lesson name real BNPL providers. Business Lab: no named providers, no switching prompts.
- Do not copy: Verdicts and advice: Finanzas para todos 'Mis deudas' grades the reader green, amber or red on age bands with no source (15%/20% under 55), headlines red with 'You have problems!' and advises cutting debt. It is advice, moralising, and colour carries the signal.
- Do not copy: Moralising defaults: 'stop smoking' and 'eat out less' as the preset savings lines (Mis caprichos). The ADEME 'do I really need this?' quiz frames spending as a moral and environmental question. Business Lab uses neutral example lines and never judges choices.
- Do not copy: Optimistic or unlabelled defaults: rent-vs-buy assumes a 5% yearly rise in house prices; several tools preset 2% interest without calling it an example. Business Lab labels every example rate in the input and in the result sentence.

### US bank and consumer-finance hubs: JPMorgan Chase Money Skills, Bank of America Better Money Habits, Wells Fargo Hands o

- Pattern: Answer first. Chase's vendor tools say 'In this tool, the results display before the inputs'. On a phone the headline answer sits above the fold and the inputs follow.
- Pattern: Open on a filled example so there is a real answer on load (Chase: $10 'less dining out' gives $120/yr; Bankrate $5,500 at 21%; NerdWallet $500 at 16.99% gives $7.03). Tools that open blank show '$0', '0 months' or 'Save less than $1/month', which reads like a broken answer (NerdWallet 50/30/20, BofA, Hands on Banking).
- Pattern: A live update with no Calculate button (Chase: sliders plus text fields; results update 'as you move the sliders or leave a text field') feels lighter than Calculate/Reset forms (Hands on Banking, Bankrate).
- Pattern: The result is one sentence with the number inside: 'You could save $120 each year'; 'It could take you 0 years and 6 months to reach your goal'; 'With a minimum payment: It will take you N months to be rid of your debt. In that time, you will pay $X in interest.'
- Pattern: A two-way solve toggle asked up front: by payment or by months (Bankrate, Capital One, Discover 'Choose how you'd like to calculate'). The Rule of 72 solves for rate or years (Hands on Banking).
- Pattern: A monthly target broken into weekly and daily amounts ('$233.78 a month… $7.69 a day or $53.80 a week', Bankrate) makes a goal concrete for readers who think in weeks.
- Pattern: Show the working. NerdWallet prints daily rate, rate factor and formula beside the interest charge. Chase's disclaimer shows (1st × 12) + (2nd × 12) + (3rd × 12) with a worked example. Bankrate explains 'payment is applied to interest first'.
- Pattern: Accessible charts: Chase's charts have 'View as data table' and an 'Enable color patterns' toggle, so identity never rests on colour alone.
- Do not copy: Rates prefilled from the provider's own product or an unlabelled number: Capital One prefills 3.00% ('The default APY… is the 360 Performance Savings rate'); Bankrate defaults to 5 with no 'example' label. Business Lab uses 0% or a rate labelled as an example, never a product rate.
- Do not copy: Product ads interleaved with calculators: NerdWallet places named bank APYs, 'Paid non-client promotion' cash accounts and 'Apply now' card offers beside its tools. Bankrate adds 'Compare today's best savings account rates' and 0% card links. NerdWallet's DTI page has a table naming lenders. Chase follows its debt result with cash-out refinance, balance transfer cards and a credit-score product. Capital One and Discover push balance transfers and pre-approval.
- Do not copy: Directive recommendations presented as tools: BofA 'Should I pay down debt, save or invest?' ('It's smart to contribute up to the amount an employer will match'); Chase's snowball/avalanche quiz that recommends a method; NerdWallet's inflation page pointing to ~10% stock returns; Hands on Banking's 'Get started investing as early as you can!'. Titles framed as the reader's decision ('Should I buy this?', 'Help me decide') break the rule that tool names describe the calculation.
- Do not copy: Investing and retirement projections bolted onto simple sums: Chase's spending-cut tool adds 'If you invest this amount, you'll accumulate… $3,240 at retirement'. Hands on Banking includes retirement and mortgage calculators. These are out of scope for 15–21 and imply returns.
- Do not copy: Personal-data gates: Citi's payoff calculator (an Envestnet MoneyGuide embed) requires first name, gender, date of birth, employment, marital status and state before any maths. BofA's spending tool asks ZIP, age and income band. BofA's savings page carries phone and email app-download fields and ZIP prompts.

## The tools index
