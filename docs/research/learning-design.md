# Evidence-based learning design for a teen/young-adult money hub (research report, 17 Sep 2026)

Method: web research only; primary sources preferred. Anything not confirmed on the cited page is marked **[unverified]**.

## A. Ranked teaching formats (with evidence)

1. **Retrieval practice (low-stakes quizzes, "check" questions)** — Dunlosky et al. 2013 rate practice testing "high utility" (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1529100612453266). Adesope et al. 2017 meta-analysis (272 effects): testing beats restudy and all comparators (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654316689306); a secondary summary reports g≈0.51 vs restudy, ≈0.93 vs no activity, multiple-choice ≈0.70 vs short-answer ≈0.48, and a 1–6-day gap before the final test (g≈0.82) beating same-day (https://theeconomyofmeaning.com/2017/03/21/important-new-meta-analysis-on-the-testing-effect-with-some-surprises/) **[numbers via secondary source]**. Donoghue & Hattie 2021 (242 studies): practice testing d=0.74 (https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/581216).
2. **Spaced repetition (Leitner-style review queue)** — Distributed practice d=0.85, the strongest of ten techniques (Donoghue & Hattie 2021). Cepeda et al. 2006 (317 experiments): optimal gap grows with the retention interval (https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CPVWR2006.html). Latimier et al. 2021: spaced vs massed retrieval g=0.74; expanding vs uniform schedules no different (g=0.03), so a simple Leitner box is fine (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8). Duolingo's Settles & Meeder 2016 used Leitner as the baseline for their half-life model; A/B test lifted engagement ~12% (https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf).
3. **Worked → faded examples (for calculations)** — Barbieri et al. 2023 meta-analysis (55 studies, 181 effects): g=0.48 on maths performance; correct examples beat incorrect/mixed; adding self-explanation prompts did *not* help (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1). **[fading benefit rests on older Renkl/Atkinson work, not re-verified]**
4. **Predict-then-reveal / productive failure** — Sinha & Kapur 2021 (53 studies): problem-solving-first g=0.36 for conceptual knowledge/transfer (0.87 after publication-bias correction), ≈0 for procedures; strongest for grades 6–10 (g=0.50), negative for grades 2–5 (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105). Brod 2021: a confident wrong prediction with immediate feedback drives encoding via surprise (https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-021-01904-1).
5. **Explorable explanations / reactive documents** — Bret Victor 2011 (https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/): warns a bare sandbox abdicates the author's duty to guide. Nicky Case: text for abstractions, interactives for processes; "start small, build big"; avoid gamification confusion (https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations/); start with a question, climb the ladder of abstraction, end in sandbox mode (https://blog.ncase.me/how-i-make-an-explorable-explanation/); patterns "Puzzle it out", "Place your bets", "Role play", "Sandbox" (https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/). Hub: https://explorabl.es/. No controlled-effect evidence for explorables as a genre **[evidence gap]**; indirect support from Noetel et al. 2022 (29 reviews/1,189 studies: signalling, contiguity, segmenting, coherence, personalisation all help — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/00346543211052329).
6. **Short simulations/serious games with reflection** — Clark et al. 2016 (57 studies): games vs non-game g=0.33; multiple sessions g=0.44 vs single session g=0.08; scaffolding g=0.58/0.48; single-player beat competitive; schematic graphics beat realistic (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654315582065). Stock Market Game RCT (2009): investor-knowledge effect sizes 0.39–0.45 (https://www.stockmarketgame.org/assets/pdf/2009_Learning_Point_Study_Brief_Report.pdf). NGPF Arcade: 12 games, 5–20 min each, each with a reflection worksheet (https://www.ngpf.org/arcade/). No peer-reviewed evaluation of NGPF games found **[absent]**. SPENT (https://playspent.org/): Roussos & Dovidio 2016 found playing it did not improve attitudes to the poor and, for low-meritocracy players, *increased* blame (https://cyberpsychology.eu/article/view/6176) — a caution for budgeting sims.
7. **Elaborative interrogation / "why?" prompts** — d=0.56 (Donoghue & Hattie 2021); Dunlosky 2013 "moderate utility".
8. **Dual coding (diagram + words)** — Imagery d=0.56 (Donoghue & Hattie 2021); Noetel 2022 supports multimedia, contiguity, signalling.
9. **Interleaving (mixed-topic review sets)** — Brunmair & Richter 2019 (238 effects): g=0.42 overall; maths g=0.34; expository text ≈0; vocabulary negative — use for discriminating similar concepts, not for definitions (https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf).
10. **Teach-back / preparing to teach** — Kobayashi 2024 (39 studies): teaching after study g=0.27; with teaching expectancy g=0.48 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-024-09871-4). EEF: peer tutoring +6 months, high evidence security (https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/peer-tutoring); collaborative learning +5 months, low security (https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/collaborative-learning-approaches).
11. **Just-in-time micro-guides tied to a real decision** — Fernandes, Lynch & Netemeyer 2014 (90 interventions): education explains 0.1% of behaviour variance and decays within ~20 months; recommend "just-in-time" content (https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2013.1849). Kaiser & Menkhoff 2017 (126 studies): works better at "teachable moments", with higher intensity; debt behaviours hardest to move (https://academic.oup.com/wber/article-abstract/31/3/611/4471971).
12. **Printable/offline worksheets for clubs** — indirect evidence only: NGPF pairs every game with a reflection sheet; EEF says peer tutoring needs structured materials. **[evidence gap]**

## B. Financial-education effectiveness (headline numbers)
- Kaiser, Lusardi, Menkhoff & Urban 2022 (76 RCTs, >160k people): knowledge +0.186 SD, behaviour +0.094 SD; ~3–5× Fernandes; no evidence of decay ≥6 months; median cost $22.90/participant (https://www.nber.org/papers/w27057; https://gflec.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/FED_GFLEC_Seminar.pdf).
- Kaiser & Menkhoff 2020 school meta-analysis (37 studies, 115k students): knowledge +0.33 SD (RCTs +0.15), behaviour +0.07 SD; more hours help with diminishing returns (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272775718306940).
- Bruhn et al. 2016 Brazil RCT (25k students): knowledge +0.25 SD, more saving/budgeting, but *more* expensive credit use (https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/app.20150149). Urban et al. 2020: well-implemented state mandates → higher credit scores, fewer defaults at 18–21 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272775718301699).
- OECD PISA 2022 (Vol IV, 2024): only ~8% of 15-year-olds reach Level 5; talking with parents about money ≈ +12 points; ~63% hold an account (https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-iv_5a849c2a-en.html). CFPB "building blocks" frame 13–21 as when knowledge becomes practically relevant (https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/youth-financial-education/learn/).

## C. Do / don't for 15–21 learners
NN/g teens (2019 update; 100 teens 13–17, 210 sites): teens underperform adults on reading, search and patience; write at ≤6th-grade level, short paragraphs, no tiny text, no "kids" wording, quizzes/voting/creation work, pointless multimedia backfires, fast load, avoid precision tapping (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-of-websites-for-teenagers/). NN/g young adults 18–25 (2016): confident but error-prone, scan not read, resent condescension, demand evidence, dismiss interactivity that isn't task-relevant (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/young-adults-ux/). No NN/g Gen-Z-specific study located **[gap]**.
- **Do**: one idea per screen; question before answer; immediate feedback; real numbers; show working; let them export/save a plan; plain, peer-toned language.
- **Don't**: walls of text; unexplained jargon; competitive leaderboards by default (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7); sims that moralise; auto-advancing carousels; colour-only charts.

## D. Template: 6–10 minute mobile lesson
Guo et al. 2014 (6.9M MOOC sessions): engagement drops after ~6 min per segment (https://learningatscale.acm.org/las2014/talks/paper_philip_guo2.pdf); microlearning units typically 3–5 min (https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1423546.pdf) — teen-specific evidence thin **[gap]**. Model on Khan Academy's mastery loop (https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery) and Brilliant's problem-first style (https://brilliant.org/faq/).

1. Hook (30 s) — scenario + "Place your bet" prediction. 2. Reveal + concept (90 s) — answer immediately; one diagram + ≤80 words. 3. Worked example (60–90 s). 4. Explorable (2–3 min) — guided prompts, then free play. 5. Faded practice (60 s). 6. Retrieval check (60 s) — 2–3 questions; wrong answers get a "why?" prompt. 7. Summary + transfer (30 s). 8. Schedule — Leitner queue at 1–6 days, then weeks; "explain it to a friend" card; printable worksheet.

## E. Accessible interactive charts
W3C WAI: complex images need short alt + long description visible to everyone (https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex). WCAG 1.4.1: never colour alone (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/use-of-color.html). Chartability: data table with every chart, keyboard + touch parity, ≤5 categories, 3:1 graphics/4.5:1 text (https://chartability.github.io/POUR-CAF/). Okabe-Ito colour-universal set (https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/).

## F. Gamification: evidence and guardrails for minors
Sailer & Homner 2020: cognitive g=0.49, motivational 0.36, behavioural 0.25 (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270). 2023 meta-analysis (35 interventions): intrinsic motivation g=0.26; leaderboards can embarrass low-ranked students (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7). Duolingo (2022 blog): 7-day streak → 3.6× more likely to finish course; explicitly cites loss aversion (https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit). Harms: 5Rights "Disrupted Childhood" 2023 — streaks, notifications, autoplay, random rewards exploit developing impulse control (https://5rightsfoundation.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/5rights_DisruptedChildhood_G.pdf). ICO Children's Code standard 13: no nudges that exploit psychological bias or extend use (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/13-nudge-techniques/). FTC 2022 dark-patterns report (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers).

Guardrails: reward learning not time-on-app; streaks optional, weekly, free freeze; no public leaderboards for under-18s; no variable rewards or purchasable boosts; natural stopping points; notifications off by default; badges for real-world actions; data minimisation; DPIA per ICO; test sims with disadvantaged teens for backfire.

**Key gaps:** no controlled studies of explorables as a format; no peer-reviewed evaluation of NGPF Arcade; teen-specific microlearning length evidence absent; NN/g has no dedicated Gen-Z study.
