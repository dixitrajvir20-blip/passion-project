## Six hours, not two

Your $60 <Term id="hourly-rate">hourly rate</Term> counts only the two hours the club sees. Behind them sit three hours of editing and one of travel, so the price has to pay for all six.

Quote one <Term id="flat-fee">flat fee</Term> for the whole job, with a written <Term id="scope-of-work">scope of work</Term>, such as 30 edited photos. Extras then get their own price.

## What comes off first

Every game spends $45 on travel, memory cards and the gallery, a <Term id="variable-cost">variable cost</Term> that comes off before you are paid.

No employer holds tax back from your own work. Once your <Term id="net-earnings">net earnings</Term>, what the work brought in minus its costs, reach $400 in a year, <Term id="self-employment-tax">self-employment tax</Term> applies at 15.3%. So set aside 15.3% of what each game leaves after its costs.
