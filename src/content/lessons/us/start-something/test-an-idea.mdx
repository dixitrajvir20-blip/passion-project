## What each reply cost

Tell people about a plan and most will say it sounds great. They mean it. But praise is free, and few people like being the one who says no.

**Being liked and being paid are different things, and only one of them buys a mower.**

So weigh each reply by what it cost the person who gave it. A promise to book one day costs nothing. A name and number on your list takes a little effort. A <Term id="order-deposit">deposit</Term> to hold a Saturday costs real money. That is the reply to trust.

## Three tests for one weekend

**Ask about the past, not the future.** Do not ask whether someone would hire you. Ask who mowed their lawn last summer, how often, and what they paid. A past choice is a fact; a promise is a guess. This is <Term id="market-research">market research</Term> at the size of one street. The Small Business Administration lists interviews and short surveys among the ways to do it.

**Pre-sell.** Show a flyer, a photo or a sample, and take bookings with a small deposit.

**Do one before you do fifty.** One lawn, one pair of sneakers, one sheet of stickers. A first version like this is a <Term id="prototype">prototype</Term>. It shows the real cost, time and faults while they are cheap to fix.

## Taking a deposit

A deposit is part of the price, paid now, for a job or a product you hand over later. Keep it small, so that saying yes is easy but not free. For a $30 mow, $10 is enough to tell a customer from a kind neighbor.

Take it in a way that leaves a record: a payment app, or cash with a written receipt. Then note who paid, for what, and which day the job is booked.

**A deposit is still the customer's money until you deliver.** If you cannot do the job, say so early and pay it back.

## Selling online: the ship date

Take orders and payment online, and a Federal Trade Commission rule covers the date you promise. It applies to most goods ordered online, by mail or by phone.

**Give a ship date you can keep.** If you state no date at all, you need good reason to believe you can ship within 30 days.

**If you will be late, tell buyers before the date.** Give them a new date and a choice: agree to wait, or cancel and get their money back quickly.

Pre-orders make this easy to miss, because the thing does not exist yet. Plan for the slow case, not the fast one.

## Reading the result

The used mower costs $180. That is a <Term id="fixed-cost">fixed cost</Term>: the same whether you mow two lawns or forty. The test lets you decide before you pay it.

A weak result is still an answer. Two deposits from forty houses cost you a weekend, not $180. Change one thing, such as the price, the flyer or the streets you try, and test again. Or drop it and keep your money.

A strong result has limits too. Six deposits mean six customers, not the whole street. Plan the first Saturdays from what the test showed, and borrow a mower until the bookings can pay for one.

## Rules to check first

Mowing lawns, flipping sneakers or selling stickers makes you <Term id="self-employed">self-employed</Term>. The IRS counts that money as taxable income, whatever your age. Once your net earnings from working for yourself reach $400 in a year, you must file a tax return, minors included. So note every payment and every cost, starting with the first deposit. Take tax questions to the IRS, not to a friend.

Food has its own rules, even at a one-day stand. The FDA writes a model code, and states, counties and cities build their own rules from it. So what a stand needs varies by state and city. Ask the organizer, or your city or county health department, before you take a single deposit.
