## Top line, bottom line

An <Term id="income-statement">income statement</Term> covers one stretch of time, here September. It starts from <Term id="revenue">revenue</Term>, the sales. Take off the <Term id="cost-of-goods-sold">cost of goods sold</Term>, what the drinks used, and <Term id="gross-profit">gross profit</Term> remains. Take off the <Term id="operating-expenses">operating expenses</Term>, the cost of running the shop, and <Term id="net-profit">net profit</Term> is left. Only that bottom line says what the month kept.

## Quiet months and money still owed

Beans and card fees are a <Term id="variable-cost">variable cost</Term>, falling with sales. Rent, insurance and booked shifts are <Term id="fixed-cost">fixed costs</Term>, owed whatever the register takes. The statement also counts a sale when the coffee is served, not when it is paid: the <Term id="accrual-method">accrual method</Term>. So profit and the account can differ, and that timing is <Term id="cash-flow">cash flow</Term>.
