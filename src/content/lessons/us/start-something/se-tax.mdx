## Nothing withheld, still owed

A job takes Social Security and Medicare, federal retirement and health programs, out of your pay: <Term id="withholding">withholding</Term>. Tutoring and reselling make you <Term id="self-employed">self-employed</Term>, so nothing comes out. Your <Term id="net-earnings">net earnings</Term> are your net profit: what came in, minus the <Term id="business-expense">business expenses</Term> of the work. Once they reach $400 in a year, <Term id="self-employment-tax">self-employment tax</Term> is due, whatever your age.

## Why about 14.13%, and when to pay it

At a job you pay 7.65%, and the employer pays 7.65% more. Working for yourself you are both, so the rate is 15.3%. The <Term id="irs">IRS</Term> applies it to 92.35% of net profit: 100% less the employer's 7.65%. That is about 14.13% of the whole.

Set that aside as payments land. Expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year, and the IRS generally looks for <Term id="estimated-tax">estimated tax</Term> on four dates. <Term id="bookkeeping">Bookkeeping</Term>, a dated line per payment and cost, gives the figure.
