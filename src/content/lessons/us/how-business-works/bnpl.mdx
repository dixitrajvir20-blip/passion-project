## Four payments is credit

At the checkout a button splits $60 into four payments of $15. The seller is still paid in full today. A lender pays it, and you pay the lender back. That is <Term id="buy-now-pay-later">buy now, pay later</Term>, or pay-in-four.

The price does not change. Splitting changes when you pay, not how much. **After the payment made at the till, $45 of that $60 is still owed.**

Most plans ask you to set up <Term id="autopay">autopay</Term> from a debit card or a bank account. Each instalment then leaves on its date by itself, whether or not the money is there.

## Who pays the plan

A lender that charges you no interest still earns. Most of what it earns comes from the other side of the sale: the seller pays a <Term id="merchant-discount-fee">merchant discount fee</Term>, a cut of each sale the plan brings in.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) collected these figures from five large lenders. In 2021 those seller fees came to 2.49% of everything bought through them. Late fees came to 0.28%.

**A price has to cover that cut, the way it covers every other cost of selling.** What the plan sells the seller is bigger orders: its own pitch to retailers quotes higher average order values and more finished checkouts.

## Three plans, one payday

One plan is easy to follow. Three at once is not. Looking at six large lenders, the CFPB found that about 63% of their borrowers had more than one loan running at the same time during 2022. That is <Term id="loan-stacking">loan stacking</Term>.

Each lender sees only its own plans. Your <Term id="credit-report">credit report</Term> may show some of them, all of them or none, because payment history is not always reported.

**So you are the only one who can add every instalment against one payday.** Three $60 purchases in a week make twelve instalments of $15, spread across six weeks.

## What a missed instalment costs

A 0% plan stays free only while every instalment clears. When one fails, the plan's own terms decide what follows, and they differ from lender to lender.

Most plans charge a <Term id="late-fee">late fee</Term>. In the CFPB's report it was often around $7 on an average loan of $135, and 10.5% of borrowers were charged at least one during 2021. One lender's policy allowed more than one fee for the same missed instalment. Your bank can add an <Term id="overdraft-fee">overdraft fee</Term> on top.

**Act before the date, not after.** An unpaid loan can be turned over to a <Term id="debt-collector">debt collector</Term> and reported to a credit bureau.

## Add it up before the next checkout

Three checks, and all of them before the button.

First, the instalments already running: what each takes, and on which date. Second, which payday each date falls in, because pay every two weeks and instalments every two weeks do not always line up. Third, what is left of that paycheck once rent and food have their share.

<Term id="cash-flow">Cash flow</Term> is the name for that timing. Money earned is not the same as money in the account on a Friday. **A plan is a loan, so it belongs on the list with everything else you owe.**
