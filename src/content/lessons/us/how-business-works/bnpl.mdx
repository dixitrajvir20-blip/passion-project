## Who pays the plan

At checkout a lender pays the seller in full, and you repay the lender in four parts. That is <Term id="buy-now-pay-later">buy now, pay later</Term>, and it is a loan. Instead of charging you interest, the common plan charges the seller a <Term id="merchant-discount-fee">merchant discount fee</Term> on each sale. In the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's 2021 figures, those fees were most of what five large lenders earned.

## Your cost is the timing

Each installment leaves by <Term id="autopay">autopay</Term> on its date, whether the money is there or not. Several plans at once is <Term id="loan-stacking">loan stacking</Term>, and it is common. No lender sees them all, and your <Term id="credit-report">credit report</Term> may not either. So you add them up against each payday. A failed payment can bring a <Term id="late-fee">late fee</Term>, plus an <Term id="overdraft-fee">overdraft fee</Term> from your bank.
