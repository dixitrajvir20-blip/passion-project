## Who is paid, and who may advise

A <Term id="referral-link">referral link</Term> pays the creator for each account opened through it. Free products and perks are payment too. The <Term id="ftc">FTC</Term> calls any such tie a <Term id="material-connection">material connection</Term>, and the post must disclose it where people will see it. A "not financial advice" line discloses nothing.

Being paid to advise you on <Term id="securities">securities</Term>, or to trade your money, needs registration. An <Term id="investment-adviser">investment adviser</Term> or broker who has it shows up free on <Term id="brokercheck">BrokerCheck</Term>. A screenshot of a registration can be faked, so search the name yourself.

## Test the size of the claim

<Term id="compound-growth">Compound growth</Term> works on the total so far, so each month's gain is taken on a bigger amount. A monthly figure multiplies over a year. At 20% a month, $1,000 × 1.2, twelve times over, is $8,916. Adding the same $200 each month gives $3,400. No ordinary saving or investing pays either. Below, the same rule at an example rate.
