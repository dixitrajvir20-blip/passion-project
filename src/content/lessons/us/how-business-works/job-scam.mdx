## Which way the money moves

A real employer pays you for hours worked. The <Term id="ftc">FTC</Term> is plain: an honest employer never asks you to pay to get a job.

A job scam needs money to move out of you first: a deposit in a <Term id="task-scam">task scam</Term>, gift card codes, or part of a check sent on. In a <Term id="reshipping">reshipping job</Term> you mail on goods, often bought with stolen cards, under your name.

Your Social Security number goes on the <Term id="w-4">W-4</Term> and <Term id="form-i-9">Form I-9</Term> after an offer. In a chat before one, it invites <Term id="identity-theft">identity theft</Term>.

## Why the check looks real

Your bank must let you use deposited money quickly. That is a deadline, not a verdict, so your <Term id="available-balance">available balance</Term> rises before the check is tested.

A <Term id="fake-check">fake check</Term> can take weeks to be found out. Then it bounces: the bank takes it all back, and what you sent on was your own money.
