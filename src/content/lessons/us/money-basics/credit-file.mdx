## What starts a file

With no card or loan, you are <Term id="credit-invisible">credit invisible</Term>: no file yet, not a bad one. A file starts when a lender reports an account to a <Term id="credit-bureau">credit bureau</Term>. A debit card lends nothing, so it reports nothing. Your <Term id="credit-report">credit report</Term> then shows the balance, the <Term id="credit-limit">credit limit</Term> and whether each payment was on time. A <Term id="credit-score">credit score</Term> is a formula run on it. Paying on time counts most; a low <Term id="credit-utilization">credit utilization</Term>, the share of the limit in use, helps too. Applying adds a <Term id="hard-inquiry">hard inquiry</Term>, which can lower a score a little; reading your own report is a <Term id="soft-inquiry">soft inquiry</Term>, which never does.

## Where interest starts

Pay the whole <Term id="statement-balance">statement balance</Term> by the due date and, on most cards, purchases cost no interest. Leave any part and the card's <Term id="apr">APR</Term> applies to it, and to new purchases from the day you make them. The account is reported either way.
