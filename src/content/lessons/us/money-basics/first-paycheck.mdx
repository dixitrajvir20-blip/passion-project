## Gross, deductions, net

Your <Term id="gross-pay">gross pay</Term> is $1,600; your account gets <Term id="net-pay">net pay</Term>, $1,317.60. Each line between them on the <Term id="pay-stub">pay stub</Term> is a <Term id="deduction">deduction</Term>: tax sent on for you, not a fee.

Social Security and Medicare taxes, together called <Term id="fica">FICA</Term>, take fixed shares of every paycheck, even when you owe no income tax.

## Why income tax can come back

Federal <Term id="withholding">withholding</Term> is an estimate, set from the <Term id="w-4">W-4</Term> you fill in on day one. It assumes you earn this much every payday all year, so a summer job can take more than you owe. Marking the form exempt stops federal withholding alone; FICA still comes off.

After the year, your employer sends a <Term id="w-2">W-2</Term>. The extra comes back only if you file a <Term id="tax-return">tax return</Term>.
