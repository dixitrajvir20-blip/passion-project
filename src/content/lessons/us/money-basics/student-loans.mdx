## A bill with a length

An aid offer lists loans next to grants and scholarships, and they can look alike. They are not. A grant is yours to keep. **A loan comes back as a bill, every month, for years.**

So read any loan as three numbers. The <Term id="principal">principal</Term> is what you borrow. The <Term id="interest">interest</Term> rate is what borrowing costs each year, on whatever you still owe. The <Term id="loan-term">loan term</Term> is how many months you take to repay.

The rate and the term set the monthly payment, and that payment is the part you live with. At 6.52% over ten years, a $10,000 loan is about $114 a month, 120 times.

## Federal and private, in plain terms

**Federal** student loans come from the US Department of Education. You apply through the <Term id="fafsa">FAFSA</Term>, the free federal aid form. Each year one rate is set for new undergraduate loans, the same for everyone. For loans first paid out from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027, it is 6.52%, fixed for the life of the loan.

**Private** student loans come from banks and other lenders. The rate depends on your credit history, and a <Term id="cosigner">co-signer</Term> with a stronger record can bring it down. It may be a <Term id="variable-rate">variable rate</Term>, so the payment can change. They also lack most federal options for tight months.

The federal Consumer Financial Protection Bureau says to use federal loans before private ones.

## Interest does not wait for graduation

Federal loan payments start after you leave school and a <Term id="grace-period">grace period</Term>, usually six months. Interest may start much sooner.

On a <Term id="subsidized-loan">subsidized loan</Term>, the government pays the interest while you are in school. On an <Term id="unsubsidized-loan">unsubsidized loan</Term>, and on private loans, it builds from the day the money is paid out. **No bill is due, but the amount you owe is growing.**

At 6.52%, a $5,000 loan builds about $326 of interest a year. Over four years of study that is about $1,304, and it keeps building through the grace period. Unpaid interest can be added to the principal at points the loan's rules set. That is <Term id="capitalization">capitalization</Term>, and after it you pay interest on the interest.

## Stretching the months

A longer term makes each payment smaller. It also keeps the balance around for longer, so more interest builds. **A smaller payment usually means a bigger total.**

New federal borrowers from July 1, 2026 get two plans. One sets fixed payments over 10, 15, 20 or 25 years, depending on how much was borrowed. The other sets the payment as a share of your income, from 1% to 10% depending on what you earn. A private loan's length is whatever its contract says.

Neither short nor long is right for everyone. The question is which payment fits your month, and what that fit costs in total.
