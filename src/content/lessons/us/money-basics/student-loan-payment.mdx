## Two kinds of money on one page

An <Term id="aid-offer">aid offer</Term> puts two kinds of money in one column. A <Term id="pell-grant">Pell Grant</Term> or a scholarship is generally yours to keep. The loans come back. On a <Term id="subsidized-loan">subsidized loan</Term>, the government pays the interest while you study. On an <Term id="unsubsidized-loan">unsubsidized loan</Term>, it builds from the day the money is paid out.

## Three numbers, not one

A loan is its <Term id="principal">principal</Term>, what you borrow; its <Term id="interest">interest</Term>, charged on what is still owed; and its <Term id="loan-term">loan term</Term>, the months you pay. Each payment covers that month's interest, and the rest pays down principal. So the payments add up to more than you borrowed.

Payments start after a <Term id="grace-period">grace period</Term>. If one will not fit, call your <Term id="loan-servicer">loan servicer</Term>, the company that collects them, before the due date. Ask about another <Term id="repayment-plan">repayment plan</Term>. A missed payment can lower your <Term id="credit-score">credit score</Term>.
