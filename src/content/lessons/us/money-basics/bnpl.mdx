## Four payments is a loan

At checkout, the button says four payments of $30 and no interest. The shop is still paid in full today. A lender pays it, and you pay the lender back. That is <Term id="buy-now-pay-later">buy now, pay later</Term>.

**After the first payment, you owe $90. That is a debt, even at 0%.**

The price does not change. Splitting it changes when you pay, not how much. Most lenders also ask you to be 18 or older, and to set up <Term id="autopay">autopay</Term> from a debit card, credit card or bank account. Each payment then leaves on its date by itself.

## Who sees the whole stack

One plan is easy to track. The trouble starts when plans pile up. In a 2025 study, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) looked at six large lenders. About 63% of their borrowers had more than one loan running at once during 2022, and a third had them with more than one lender. That is <Term id="loan-stacking">loan stacking</Term>.

Most of those loans never showed up in credit records. **So each lender may see only its own plans, and your <Term id="credit-report">credit report</Term> may show none of them.** Some lenders report, and others may not.

The one person sure to see every plan is you. Before adding one, add up what the running plans take this month.

## What is left each month

Say you take home $800 a month and three plans are running. This month each takes two payments: $60, $60 and $80.

**Plan payments are fixed, like rent. They leave on their dates, whatever else the month brings.** What shrinks is the money for wants and savings.

Watch the calendar too. Plan payments come every two weeks, but many paychecks come once a month. A plan started on the 1st takes payments on the 1st, 15th and 29th. So some months carry three payments from one plan.

## When a payment fails

A 0% plan stays free only while every payment goes through. If pay lands after a due date, a payment can fail. That timing gap is <Term id="cash-flow">cash flow</Term>.

A failed payment can bring a <Term id="late-fee">late fee</Term>. In a 2022 CFPB study, about one user in ten was charged at least one in 2021. Some lenders added more than one fee per missed payment. Your bank may add an <Term id="overdraft-fee">overdraft fee</Term> too.

**Act before the date, not after.** Ask the lender what can change, and move money if you can. An unpaid plan can lead to a frozen account, a debt collector and a mark on your credit report. Each plan's terms set its fees, and rules vary by state.
