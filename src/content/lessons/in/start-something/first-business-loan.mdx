## Two offers for the same ₹1,50,000

The room needs desks, chairs and a projector, and the quotes come to ₹1,50,000. A bank branch takes forms and a few days, and puts the cost in writing before anything is signed. An app will send the money tonight.

Both hand over the same ₹1,50,000. What differs is the price, and the rulebook the lender works under.

**Read both offers as one question: what does this money cost, and for how long.**

## What Udyam registration is

<Term id="udyam-registration">Udyam registration</Term> is the government's record of a business as an enterprise. It is done online, on the portal run by the Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises, and it carries no fee. Nothing is payable to anyone for it.

Registration is self-declared and paperless, so no document is uploaded. An Aadhaar number and a <Term id="pan">PAN</Term> are enough to start, and the figures for investment and turnover are taken from government records.

Size sets the category. Under limits set in March 2025, a business is a <Term id="micro-enterprise">micro enterprise</Term> while investment stays within ₹2.5 crore and turnover within ₹10 crore. **A coaching centre with two rooms sits far inside that.**

## What the certificate opens

The certificate does not approve a loan. It identifies the enterprise, and rules are written around that identity.

Banks are guided by the classification on the certificate when a loan is counted towards <Term id="priority-sector-lending">priority sector lending</Term>. Loans to micro, small and medium enterprises can all count towards it.

RBI also tells banks not to take <Term id="collateral">collateral</Term> on loans up to ₹10 lakh to micro and small enterprises. **Registration is what puts an enterprise inside those rules.**

## How a government scheme works

A <Term id="government-scheme">government scheme</Term> is a programme with written rules: who qualifies, what they get, and how to apply. Those rules sit on an official page.

Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana is one example. It lends up to ₹20 lakh without collateral to micro and small businesses that are not companies and not farms. Four bands split it by size, and ₹1,50,000 falls in the Kishor band, above ₹50,000 and up to ₹5 lakh.

Banks lend under it, and so do regional rural banks, small finance banks, finance companies and microfinance institutions. **A lender decides each application, so the scheme's own page is where to start.**

## The Key Facts Statement

A retail or small-business term loan sanctioned since 1 October 2024 comes with a <Term id="key-facts-statement">Key Facts Statement</Term>, handed to you before you sign. It carries the <Term id="apr">APR</Term>: the yearly cost of the credit, with the interest rate and every charge in one figure. Charges left off it cannot be added later without your explicit consent.

Digital loans have their own RBI directions, from 8 May 2025. Each lender sets a cooling-off period, never shorter than a day, in which a new loan can be exited without penalty. Lenders also report their loan apps to RBI, which publishes what they report.

**An instalment shown with no Key Facts Statement does not meet that rule.**

## Read a loan as three numbers

Any loan answers three questions. What comes out each month, how many months it runs, and what it adds up to in the end.

The monthly figure is the <Term id="emi">EMI</Term>. What you pay above the ₹1,50,000 is <Term id="interest">interest</Term>, and that is the number two offers should be compared on.

A smaller monthly payment is not a cheaper loan. It usually means more months, and more months of interest on the money still owed.

**Write the payment, the months and the total for both offers, side by side, before anyone signs anything.**
