## Two offers for the same ₹1,50,000

The room needs desks, chairs and a projector, and the quotes come to ₹1,50,000. A bank branch will lend it after forms, a few days and a written statement of the cost. An app will send the money tonight.

Both hand over the same ₹1,50,000. What differs is the price, and the rulebook the lender works under.

**Read both offers as one question: what does this money cost, and for how long.**

## What Udyam registration is

<Term id="udyam-registration">Udyam registration</Term> is the government's record of a business as an enterprise. It is done on the Ministry of MSME's portal, online and free. The portal says: "Registration Process is totally free. No Costs or Fees are to be paid to anyone."

Registration is self-declared. It starts from an Aadhaar number, with PAN and GSTIN where they apply, and nothing is uploaded. Investment and turnover are read from the income tax and GST systems.

Size sets the category. Since 1 April 2025 a business is a <Term id="micro-enterprise">micro enterprise</Term> while investment stays within ₹2.5 crore and turnover within ₹10 crore. **A coaching centre with two rooms sits far inside that.**

## What the certificate opens

The certificate does not approve a loan. It identifies the enterprise, and several rules are written around that identity.

Banks follow the classification on the certificate when they decide whether a loan counts towards priority sector lending. That is the share of credit banks must direct to certain borrowers, and micro enterprises have their own sub-target inside it.

RBI also tells banks not to take <Term id="collateral">collateral</Term> on loans up to ₹10 lakh to micro and small enterprises. **Registration is what puts an enterprise inside those rules.**

## A scheme is a rulebook, not an offer

A <Term id="government-scheme">government scheme</Term> is a programme with written rules: who qualifies, what they get, and how to apply. Those rules sit on an official page.

Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana is one example. It covers non-corporate, non-farm micro and small businesses, with no collateral, up to ₹20 lakh. Four categories split it by size, and ₹1,50,000 falls in the Kishor band, above ₹50,000 and up to ₹5 lakh.

Lending is done by banks, regional rural banks, small finance banks, non-banking financial companies and microfinance institutions. **The lender decides each application against the scheme's rules, so the scheme's own page is where to start.**

## The Key Facts Statement

Since 1 October 2024 a regulated lender must give you a <Term id="key-facts-statement">Key Facts Statement</Term> before a retail or MSME term loan is signed. It carries the <Term id="apr">APR</Term>: the yearly cost of the credit, with the interest rate and every charge in one figure. Charges left off it cannot be added later without your explicit consent.

Digital loans have their own RBI directions, from 8 May 2025. They give at least one day to exit a new loan without penalty. Lenders report their lending apps to RBI, which publishes the list.

**An app that shows an instalment but no Key Facts Statement is the warning.**

## Read a loan as three numbers

Any loan answers three questions. What comes out each month, how many months it runs, and what it adds up to in the end.

The monthly figure is the <Term id="emi">EMI</Term>. What you pay above the ₹1,50,000 is <Term id="interest">interest</Term>, and that is the number two offers should be compared on.

A smaller monthly payment is not a cheaper loan. It usually means more months, and more months of interest on the money still owed.

**Write the payment, the months and the total for both offers, side by side, before anyone signs anything.**
