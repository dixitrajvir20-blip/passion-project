  - "Accountants call the two readings the accrual basis and the cash basis.   - "A month's profit statement follows the accrual basis, which is why all 24 fees count this month. A cash plan follows the dates on which money actually moves."

## Two readings of one month

Your 24 students pay ₹1,500 each: ₹36,000 of <Term id="revenue">revenue</Term>. Take off every cost and ₹10,800 is <Term id="profit">profit</Term>. It counts each fee earned this month, whenever it is paid, and has no dates.

## Why the 5th comes up short

The account works by dates. Twelve families pay by the 3rd, twelve on the 20th. The print shop takes ₹7,200 on the 2nd; three bills fall due on the 5th. On the 5th only the early fees can pay them.

The timing of money in and out is <Term id="cash-flow">cash flow</Term>. Money kept back to bridge such a gap is <Term id="working-capital">working capital</Term>: it is not spare.
