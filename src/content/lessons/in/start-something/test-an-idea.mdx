## Praise is free

Tell people about a plan and most will say it sounds great. They mean it. But a kind word costs them nothing, and few people enjoy being the one who says no.

**A compliment tells you people like you. It does not tell you they will pay.**

So judge every reply by what it cost the person who gave it. A promise to buy one day costs nothing. A name and phone number costs a little effort. A <Term id="pre-order">pre-order</Term> with a small advance costs real money, and that is the reply to trust.

## Three tests for one weekend

**Ask about the past, not the future.** Do not ask whether someone would buy. Ask what they did last time: where they ate at the last fest, and what it cost. A past choice is a fact, while a future promise is a guess. This is <Term id="market-research">market research</Term> on a small scale. CBSE's Class 11 Entrepreneurship course covers it, and Class 12 has a market survey project.

**Pre-sell.** Show a photo or a sample, and take orders with a small advance.

**Make one before you make fifty.** One <Hi>rakhi</Hi>, one batch of ten cups, one trial class. A first version like this, a <Term id="prototype">prototype</Term>, shows what the thing really costs and how long it takes.

## Taking an advance

An advance is part of the price, paid now, for something you hand over later. Keep it small, so that saying yes is easy but not free. For a ₹40 cup, ₹20 is enough to tell a real buyer from a polite one.

Take it by <Term id="upi">UPI</Term> to your UPI ID or QR code, so each payment arrives with a name and a time. Then write down who paid, for what, and when they collect.

**An advance is the buyer's money until you deliver.** If you cannot make the thing, say so early and send the money back.

## Reading the result

Booking the stall costs ₹1,500, a <Term id="fixed-cost">fixed cost</Term> you pay whether ten people come or two hundred. The test is there so you decide before you pay it.

A weak result is still a result. Two advances from forty people asked cost you a weekend, not ₹1,500. Change one thing, such as the price, the snack or the people you asked, and test again. Or drop it and keep your money.

A strong result has limits too. Fourteen advances mean fourteen buyers, not three hundred. Plan the first batch from what the test showed.

## If the idea is food

Food has rules, even at a stall that is only up for the fest. The national food safety authority lists temporary stall holders among the small sellers who need <Term id="fssai-registration">FSSAI registration</Term>. Ask the fest committee what they need from food stalls before you take a single advance.
