## What one cover leaves behind

At a <Term id="wholesale">wholesale</Term> market, traders sell phone covers in bulk to shops and stalls. You buy them there at ₹80 each. At the Sunday market, they sell for ₹150. So each sale leaves ₹70 with you.

That ₹70 is your <Term id="margin">margin</Term>. For a reseller, the ₹80 you paid is the <Term id="variable-cost">variable cost</Term>. Sell ten covers and they cost you ₹800. Sell a hundred and they cost ₹8,000.

**Every other bill the stall has must be paid out of that ₹70.**

## The month gets paid first

Some costs arrive whatever you sell. Your spot at the market is ₹500 a Sunday, so ₹2,000 for four Sundays. A folding table and umbrella rent for ₹1,000 a month. One auto trip a month to fetch new covers costs ₹500. Together that is ₹3,500. These are your <Term id="fixed-cost">fixed costs</Term>.

**Each ₹70 goes to the ₹3,500 before any of it is yours.** The 50th cover pays the last of it. That cover is your <Term id="break-even">break-even</Term> point: no loss, and no profit yet.

Each month starts again from zero. A good November does not pay for December's spot.

## After the 50th cover

This is the part people miss. **After break-even, each cover adds its whole ₹70 to your <Term id="profit">profit</Term>.** Nothing more comes out of it, because the month is already paid.

So a month of 80 covers does not make 80 lots of ₹70. The first 50 paid the bills. Only the 30 after them count, and 30 lots of ₹70 is ₹2,100.

Below break-even, the same sum runs backwards. Stop at 40 covers and you are 10 short of the 50, which leaves you ₹700 down.

## Can you sell that many?

Break-even is a target, so test it before you buy anything. Fifty covers over four Sundays is about 13 each Sunday. Stand at the market for an hour and count how many people stop at stalls like yours.

Say your honest guess is 20 a Sunday, or 80 a month. Then 50 of those 80 only pay the bills. One rainy Sunday cuts your profit from ₹2,100 to ₹700.

Model project reports from a government institute for small businesses run the same test. One, for a phone repair shop, puts break-even at under a quarter of a full year's work.

## Covers in the bag are money already spent

Break-even counts covers sold, not covers bought. Covers you have bought but not sold are <Term id="stock">stock</Term>. You paid for them in cash on the day you bought them.

Buy 100 and sell 60, and the 40 left hold ₹3,200 of your money. That month shows ₹700 of profit, yet your cash is ₹2,500 lower than when it began. The ₹3,200 comes back only as those covers sell, and covers for a phone people stop using may never sell.

**Profit tells you how the month went. The bag tells you where your cash is.**
