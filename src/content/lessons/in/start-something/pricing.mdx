## Start from what one tiffin costs you

Before you look at anyone else's price, look at one tiffin. Rice, dal, a sabzi, four rotis, the oil and gas to cook them, and the box it goes in. Call it ₹55. That is the <Term id="variable-cost">variable cost</Term>: every extra tiffin adds another ₹55.

Gas and the box are the costs people forget. **Count everything one tiffin uses up, not just the food.**

Adding up your costs first, then putting an amount on top, is <Term id="cost-plus-pricing">cost-plus pricing</Term>. It gives you a floor. Sell below ₹55 and every tiffin loses money, however many you sell.

## What the price has to cover

₹55 is only the first job the price does. Selling cooked food is a food business, so you get an <Term id="fssai-registration">FSSAI registration</Term> for a small fee. Then come the phone plan you take orders on and the instalment on a big cooker and delivery bags. Together, these <Term id="fixed-cost">fixed costs</Term> come to ₹2,400 a month.

Sell 150 tiffins a month, and each one carries ₹16 of that. Then there is your own time: the mornings cooking and the walk to the hostel. **At ₹80 a tiffin, the ₹9 left over is what pays you.**

## Why the mess can charge ₹65

The mess has different costs. It buys rice by the sack and cooks 200 plates in the same big pots. Family members may work there without a wage. Its plate may be smaller than yours. Or it is losing money and has not noticed yet.

**You can copy a price only if you can copy the costs behind it.** Undercut the mess at ₹60, and your <Term id="margin">margin</Term> is ₹5 a tiffin. Your <Term id="break-even">break-even</Term> jumps to 480 tiffins a month. That is 16 a day, every day, cooked and carried by one person.

## Compete on what the mess cannot copy

If you cannot be the cheapest, be worth the extra ₹15. Students pay more for what saves them trouble. Lunch at the hostel gate at 1 pm. Food cooked that morning, with less oil. The same time every day, exam weeks included.

**Reliability is part of what you sell.** A student let down once at 1 pm starts looking at the mess again.

A monthly plan paid on the 1st helps your <Term id="cash-flow">cash flow</Term> too: the money for rice arrives before the rice is bought.

Your costs set the lowest price you can charge. What the buyer gets sets the most they will pay. The mess's ₹65 is simply the number your tiffin has to explain.
