## What one student's fee leaves behind

Two graduates rent a room near a bus stop and teach maths and physics to Class 11 and 12 students. Each student pays ₹1,500 a month.

Not all of that stays with the centre. Every student gets printed notes, a weekly test paper and a folder. That is about ₹300 a month, spent on that one student. It is a <Term id="variable-cost">variable cost</Term>: twenty students need twenty sets of paper.

What is left from one fee is the <Term id="contribution-margin">contribution</Term>: ₹1,200 towards everything else.

**Contribution is the fee minus what that one student uses up, not the whole fee.**

## The room is paid before anyone is taught

Some costs arrive whether ten students come or thirty. Rent is ₹12,000 a month. Electricity runs to about ₹1,500. The projector was bought on an instalment of ₹4,500 a month. Together those are ₹18,000 of <Term id="fixed-cost">fixed costs</Term>.

Each ₹1,200 of contribution goes to that ₹18,000 first. The fifteenth fee finishes paying it. That student is <Term id="break-even">break-even</Term>: no loss left, and nothing yet to keep.

**Every month starts again at zero.** A full March does not pay April's rent.

## After the fifteenth student

Past break-even nothing comes out of a fee except that student's ₹300 of material. **Each student after the fifteenth adds ₹1,200 to <Term id="profit">profit</Term>.**

So a month of twenty students is not twenty lots of ₹1,200. Fifteen of them paid for the room. The five after them are the ₹6,000 of profit.

Under the line the same sum runs backwards. A month that ends at twelve students is three short, so it closes ₹3,600 down.

## A lower fee needs more students than it looks

Empty seats make a fee cut tempting. Drop the fee to ₹1,200 and the contribution drops to ₹900, while the room still costs ₹18,000. The month now needs twenty students, not fifteen.

A fifth off the fee asks for a third more students, and the room has to seat them all.

The cut also reaches every student already enrolled, from the month it starts. The extra students are only expected. **Test a lower fee against the count it creates, not against the centre two streets away.**

## What the count leaves out

Three things, and the first is the largest. Neither founder draws a wage, so no pay sits inside the ₹18,000. Put what each of you needs into the fixed costs, and the count rises well above fifteen.

Second, the room has a seat limit. A break-even the room cannot hold is not a plan.

Third, a one-off cost is not a monthly cost. The whiteboard was bought once. <Term id="udyam-registration">Udyam registration</Term>, which records the centre as a service enterprise, is free of charge on the ministry's portal. Neither changes the monthly count.
