## From ₹35,000 to ₹30,600

<Term id="ctc">CTC</Term> means cost to company: what the job is expected to cost the company over a year. Divided by 12, this offer is ₹35,000 a month. What reaches your bank account is your <Term id="in-hand-pay">in-hand pay</Term>, here ₹30,600.

The ₹4,400 in between is not a fee. Some of it is paid for you into a fund, and some goes to a state tax. Each part is named on your <Term id="payslip">payslip</Term>: three lines that build your pay, and three taken off it.

**Plan the month on in-hand pay, because that is the part you can spend.**

## What the CTC counts that your pay does not

A CTC adds up everything the company expects to spend on you. The employer's provident fund share of ₹2,100 a month goes straight to the fund, so it is counted in the CTC and never appears in your salary.

That is why this payslip starts from ₹32,900 of <Term id="gross-pay">gross salary</Term> rather than ₹35,000.

Letters often count <Term id="gratuity">gratuity</Term> or health cover inside the CTC as well. Both are real, and neither arrives as monthly pay.

## Both provident fund shares are 12% of wages

<Term id="epf">EPF</Term>, the Employees' Provident Fund, takes 12% of your wages each month, and the employer puts in 12% as well. On this payslip your wages are the <Term id="basic-pay">basic pay</Term> of ₹17,500, so each share is ₹2,100.

Part of the employer's share funds the <Term id="eps">Employees' Pension Scheme</Term> rather than your own account. Interest is added each year: the board that runs the scheme kept the rate at 8.25% for 2025-26, subject to the Finance Ministry agreeing.

**Both shares are savings held in your name, not a charge for being employed.**

## Who is in the fund, and when you can draw on it

Membership is compulsory for employees of covered workplaces, up to a wage ceiling. That ceiling was ₹15,000 a month from September 2014. It rose to ₹25,000 from 17 September 2026.

Wages of ₹17,500 sit under the new figure, so the deductions on this payslip are the ordinary ones.

Under the 2026 scheme rules the money can be drawn early for illness, education, marriage or housing. That needs a year of service, and a quarter of the balance has to stay in the account.

## Professional tax and TDS

<Term id="professional-tax">Professional tax</Term> is charged by the state you work in, under a law that state passes for itself. The amount differs from one state to the next. The Constitution caps it at ₹2,500 a year. The ₹200 here is an example.

<Term id="tds">TDS</Term>, tax deducted at source, is income tax your employer takes month by month. It is worked out from the income and tax regime expected for the whole year. Twelve months of ₹32,900 is ₹3,94,800. Under the new regime, the default one, income up to ₹4,00,000 is not taxed for assessment year 2026-27, so this line reads ₹0. A rebate holds the tax at nil further up, to ₹12,00,000 of taxable income.

## Read the payslip in the first week

Three checks, while the month is still fresh. First, that basic pay and the allowances match the breakup in your offer letter. Second, that every deduction has a name you can explain. Third, that the EPF amount is 12% of the basic pay shown above it.

If a line has no name you recognise, ask the payroll team what it is. Then give the ₹30,600 its jobs before the first spend, because a plan built on ₹35,000 runs ₹4,400 short every month.
