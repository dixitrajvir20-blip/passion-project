## From ₹35,000 to ₹30,600

<Term id="ctc">CTC</Term> means cost to company: what the job is expected to cost the company over a year. Divided by 12, this offer is ₹35,000 a month. What reaches your bank account is your <Term id="in-hand-pay">in-hand pay</Term>, here ₹30,600.

The ₹4,400 in between is not a fee. Some of it is paid for you into a fund, and some goes to a state tax. Each part is named on your <Term id="payslip">payslip</Term>, which lists six lines between the two totals.

**Plan the month on in-hand pay, because that is the part you can spend.**

## What the CTC counts that your pay does not

A CTC adds up everything the company expects to spend on you. The employer's provident fund share of ₹2,100 a month goes straight to the fund, so it is counted in the CTC and never appears in your salary.

That is why this payslip starts from ₹32,900 of <Term id="gross-pay">gross salary</Term> rather than ₹35,000.

Letters often count <Term id="gratuity">gratuity</Term> or health cover inside the CTC as well. Both are real, and neither arrives as monthly pay.

## Both provident fund shares are 12% of basic pay

<Term id="epf">EPF</Term>, the Employees' Provident Fund, takes 12% of your <Term id="basic-pay">basic pay</Term> each month, and the employer contributes 12% as well. Here basic pay is ₹17,500, so each share is ₹2,100.

Of the employer's side, 8.33% of wages goes to the <Term id="eps">Employees' Pension Scheme</Term> and the rest into your provident fund account. Interest is added to the account each year, at a rate the scheme's board recommends and the government notifies: 8.25% for 2025-26.

**The money is held for you, not taken from you.** The scheme allows withdrawals for needs such as illness, education, marriage or a house.

## Who has to be in the fund

EPF is compulsory for employees of covered workplaces drawing wages up to ₹15,000 a month, the scheme's ceiling since September 2014. Someone joining above that figure is not covered automatically, though an employer can still run it for them, as this payslip does.

On 16 September 2026 the Union Cabinet approved raising that ceiling to ₹25,000 a month, which would bring the band between the two figures into mandatory cover. Until the scheme rules change, your own payslip is the record of what is deducted.

## Professional tax and TDS

<Term id="professional-tax">Professional tax</Term> is charged by the state you work in, under that state's own law, so the amount differs from one state to the next. The ₹200 here is an example.

<Term id="tds">TDS</Term>, tax deducted at source, is income tax the employer takes month by month from the income you are expected to earn for the year. Twelve months of ₹32,900 is ₹3,94,800. Under the new regime, which is the default, income up to ₹4,00,000 is not taxed for assessment year 2026-27, so this line reads ₹0.

## Read the payslip in the first week

Three checks, while the month is still fresh. First, that basic pay and the allowances match the breakup in your offer letter. Second, that every deduction has a name you can explain. Third, that the EPF amount is 12% of the basic pay shown above it.

If a line has no name you recognise, ask the payroll team what it is. Then give the ₹30,600 its jobs before the first spend, because a plan built on ₹35,000 runs ₹4,400 short every month.
