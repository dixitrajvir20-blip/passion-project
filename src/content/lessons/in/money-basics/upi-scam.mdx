## The PIN has one job

Your <Term id="upi-pin">UPI PIN</Term> does one thing: it approves money going out. **If a screen asks for your PIN so that you can receive money, the money is about to leave.**

Receiving needs nothing from you. The payer needs only your UPI ID or number. No PIN, no scan, no new app, no call.

NPCI, which runs <Term id="upi">UPI</Term>, says the same: the PIN is only for money leaving your account, and a QR code only for paying. Some apps now take a fingerprint or face check instead of the PIN. Same job, same rule.

## One trick, many costumes

Every pattern here is a way to get that approval from you, or to watch you give it.

The oldest is the <Term id="collect-request">collect request</Term>: a bill made to look like a payment. Since 1 October 2025, UPI no longer processes requests sent from one person to another. That covers person-to-person requests only, so one can still reach you under a business name. **Whatever the costume, ask one thing: am I trying to pay this person?**

The others swap the prop. A refund QR. A KYC link with a deadline. A helper app that shows your screen. A caller in uniform demanding a transfer. A job that pays you to pass money along, which makes you a <Term id="money-mule">money mule</Term>.

## On a shared or borrowed phone

Many people pay from a phone that is not only theirs: a parent's, a sibling's, the family's. That changes where the PIN is weakest.

**On a shared phone, the PIN lives only in your head.** Not in notes, not in a chat to yourself, not in a photo. Keep the payment app's own lock switched on, and close the app when you finish. If your account is set up on a friend's phone, remove it before handing the phone back.

Type the PIN only on the app's own PIN page, with the screen turned away. At a busy <Hi>kirana</Hi> counter, a PIN can be read over a shoulder. If that happens, change it the same day.

## If money has already gone

Speed is the part you control. **Call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in as soon as you notice, before anything else.** The report goes on to the banks, so the money can be blocked before it moves again. That raises the chance of getting it back. It is not a promise.

Keep these ready: the transaction ID, the date, the bank's name, your UPI ID and a screenshot. An acknowledgement number then arrives by SMS. Use it to finish the complaint on the portal within 24 hours.

Then tell your bank through its app or the number on your card, and change your PIN. These scripts are rehearsed, and they work on careful people too. Reporting one is ordinary.
