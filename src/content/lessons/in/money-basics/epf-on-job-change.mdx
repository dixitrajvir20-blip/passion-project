## An account that follows you

<Term id="epf">EPF</Term> is tied to you rather than to one employer. Your <Term id="uan">UAN</Term> links every provident fund account you hold, so the same number follows you to the next job.

Changing jobs does not close the old account. It stops receiving contributions, and the balance sits there until you move it or claim it.

**Moving it is a <Term id="transfer-claim">transfer claim</Term>, filed as Form 13 on EPFO's member portal.** EPFO said in April 2025 that the destination office no longer approves these claims. Once the office holding the old account approves, the balance is credited to the new account.

## What goes in each month

Two shares go in every month, and both are worked out on your <Term id="basic-pay">basic pay</Term>, not on the whole salary. You put in 12%. Your employer puts in 12% as well.

The employer's half is then split. Part of it funds the <Term id="employees-pension-scheme">Employees' Pension Scheme</Term>, which pays a pension later instead of a balance you can draw on. **That part is 8.33% of wages, counted only up to a <Term id="wage-ceiling">wage ceiling</Term>.** The other 3.67% stays in the provident fund beside your own 12%.

The ceiling was ₹15,000 a month, and it rose to ₹25,000 from 17 September 2026. The pay in the examples below sits under both figures.

## The rate is declared a year at a time

The rate is not fixed in advance. EPFO's Central Board of Trustees recommends one each year, and the government notifies it before it is credited to accounts.

For 2025-26 the board recommended 8.25% at its meeting on 2 March 2026. **The rate is set one year at a time, so it records that year and promises nothing about the next.**

## A withdrawal before five years

Schedule XI of the Income-tax Act, 2025 leaves the balance out of your income after five years or more of <Term id="continuous-service">continuous service</Term>. Ill health, the closure of the employer's business and other causes beyond your control are treated the same way.

Below five years, without one of those reasons, that exclusion does not apply and the balance is taxable. An assessing officer works the tax out across the years the balance built up, rather than as one year's income.

**Section 392 also has 10% deducted at source where the payment is ₹50,000 or more.** That deduction is <Term id="tds">TDS</Term>, and your return settles the rest.

## A transfer, and the clock

A transfer does two things at once. The amount moved into the new employer's recognised fund stays out of your income, and the months already served keep counting.

The second part is paragraph 8 of the same schedule: service with a previous employer is added in, once the balance has come across from their fund.

**So the two answers HR offers are different transactions.** Which one suits your year is yours to weigh, and the tax rule above is what separates them.
