## One number, one record

<Term id="epf">EPF</Term> follows the person, not the employer. The number that identifies you, your <Term id="uan">UAN</Term>, is issued once and stays yours at every job after it.

Changing jobs does not close the old account. It stops receiving contributions, and the balance sits there until you move it or claim it.

**Moving it is a <Term id="transfer-claim">transfer claim</Term>, which credits the old balance into the account at the new job.** EPFO said in April 2025 that it had taken the employer's approval out of most of these claims. The balance is credited at the receiving office once the old office approves.

## What went in each month

Two shares go in every month, and both are worked out on your <Term id="basic-pay">basic pay</Term> rather than on the whole salary. You put in 12%. Your employer puts in 12% as well.

The employer's half is then split. Part of it funds the <Term id="employees-pension-scheme">Employees' Pension Scheme</Term>, which pays a pension later instead of a balance you can draw on. **In a written reply in July 2025, the labour ministry put that pension share at 8.33% of wages.**

The rest, 3.67%, lands in the provident fund beside your own 12%.

## Interest is declared a year at a time

The rate is not fixed in advance. EPFO's Central Board of Trustees recommends one each year, and the government notifies it before it is credited to accounts.

For 2025-26 the board recommended 8.25% at its meeting on 2 March 2026. **A rate set one year at a time is a record of that year, not a promise about the next.**

The calculator below grows a monthly amount at that rate, to show the shape of it. Your own passbook shows what was credited to you.

## A withdrawal before five years

Schedule XI of the Income-tax Act, 2025 leaves the balance out of your income once you have five years or more of <Term id="continuous-service">continuous service</Term>. Ill health, the closure of the employer's business and other causes beyond your control are treated the same way.

Below five years, without one of those reasons, the balance is counted in your income for the year instead. **Section 392 then has 10% deducted at source where the payment is ₹50,000 or more.** That deduction is <Term id="tds">TDS</Term>, and your return settles the rest.

## A transfer, and the clock

A transfer does two things at once. The amount moved into the new employer's recognised fund stays out of your income, and the months already served keep counting.

The second part is paragraph 8 of the same schedule: service with a previous employer is added in, once the balance has come across from their fund.

**So the two answers HR offers are different transactions, not two routes to the same place.** Which one suits your year is yours to weigh, and the tax rule above is what separates them.

## Read your own record

The member portal shows the passbook under your UAN: what went in, what interest was credited, and which accounts are still sitting apart.

EPFO also said in April 2025 that a transfer certificate can be downloaded there. It carries the balance with interest, the service history used for the pension, and the employment details behind both.

**When two jobs sit under one UAN and the balance has moved, one page holds the record.** Where a second UAN exists, that is the thing to raise with EPFO first.
