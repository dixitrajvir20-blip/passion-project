## Give every rupee a job

Pocket money, tuition money, a stipend: most first earnings land by <Term id="upi">UPI</Term>, with no plan attached. Money like that gets spent on whatever comes first. A snack, a recharge, a friend's birthday. None of it feels big, and by the 12th the balance is zero.

A <Term id="budget">budget</Term> changes the order of events. **You decide where the money goes before it arrives, not after it has gone.** Each rupee gets one of four jobs: needs, home, wants or savings.

Writing down what you spent still helps, because it shows whether the plan matched the month. But a record looks back. The plan is what moves the 12th.

## Needs, wants and the home line

<Term id="needs-and-wants">Needs and wants</Term> are two of the jobs. Needs are the costs that come back every month whatever you do: bus fare, recharge, notebooks, exam fees. Wants are what you choose: food out, clothes, a game, a gift. Wants are a real job too, and a plan with nothing for them is hard to keep.

Then there is home. In many Indian families, part of what you earn goes to the household: the <Hi>kirana</Hi> bill, a sibling's fees, the electricity. **If money goes home, give it a line of its own, because a plan that ignores it is not a plan.** For some people that line is zero. For others it is the biggest one.

## A starting split, not a rule

You may have heard of 50/30/20: half for needs, 30% for wants, 20% for savings. It is a common guideline and a starting point, not a rule. It has no home line. This split does.

| Savings | 20% | ₹600 |

**The percentages are yours to change.** A hostel student may need 60% for needs. Someone who gives half at home has a smaller wants line. The test is the same: needs covered, home given its share, something kept back.

## Savings move first

The savings line works best when it moves on the day the money lands, before the first spend. The Reserve Bank of India's awareness booklet puts saving in the same place in the order: first, ahead of any spending.

The first job of that money is an <Term id="emergency-fund">emergency fund</Term>, for the cracked phone screen, the lost bus pass, the fee nobody mentioned. **Without that buffer, one surprise becomes a loan, and next month starts short.**

Keep it somewhere you cannot spend by accident. Where exactly is your choice, and this lesson does not pick one for you.
