## What a buffer is

A <Term id="emergency-fund">buffer</Term> is one month of your own costs, kept where you can reach it. Not a figure from someone else's life: your room, your food, your travel, your phone.

Add up last month. Here it comes to ₹5,500.

| Last month | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Room share | ₹2,500 |
| Food | ₹1,800 |
| Travel | ₹600 |
| Phone and data | ₹300 |
| Course materials | ₹300 |
| One month of costs | ₹5,500 |

**Write it as a number and a date.** ₹5,500, filled by the third payment of ₹2,000. A target with no date stays a plan.

In 2019 the NCFE asked adults whether they could pay a major expense the size of one month's income without borrowing. Across India, 45% said yes.

## Growth on growth

Money left in an account can earn <Term id="interest">interest</Term>, a payment for leaving it there. The amount you started with is the <Term id="principal">principal</Term>.

Leave the interest in, and the next round of interest is worked out on the larger amount. That is <Term id="compound-growth">compound growth</Term>.

Over 12 months it is a small effect. ₹2,000 a month at an illustrative 6% a year adds ₹671 to ₹24,000 of deposits. At 0% the same deposits stay ₹24,000.

**It is not ₹25,440, which is 6% on the whole ₹24,000.** Each deposit earns only for the months it has been there, and the last one has been there for days.

## The same maths on what you owe

Interest compounds against a borrower the same way. A balance of ₹10,000 at 36% a year, charged monthly, is charged 3% each month on what is owed by then.

Repay nothing for 12 months and it is about ₹14,260. One flat 36% charge on ₹10,000 would be ₹3,600, so the extra ₹660 is interest charged on interest.

| ₹10,000 owed, nothing repaid | After 12 months |
| --- | --- |
| At 36% a year, charged monthly | about ₹14,260 |
| One flat charge of 36% | ₹13,600 |

Before you sign, an RBI-regulated lender must hand you a <Term id="key-facts-statement">Key Facts Statement</Term> carrying the <Term id="apr">APR</Term> and every charge. **Read the total you repay against what you borrowed.**

## The rate is an assumption

Every rate here is a figure typed into a calculator, not an offer. Rates on accounts change, and a higher rate usually carries more risk, including getting back less than you put in.

Prices move too. That is <Term id="inflation">inflation</Term>, and a rough check is to take the rise in prices away from the growth rate.

**A buffer is not sized to grow. It is sized to be there on the Tuesday the screen breaks.**

## Where a buffer can sit

Money you may need this week has to be reachable this week. This lesson does not pick a place for you, and the choice is yours to make.

One rule is worth knowing. RBI's 2012 circular says a Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account carries no minimum balance requirement, and that banks are to treat it as a normal service open to all. A small balance does not have to cost a penalty.
