## What a buffer is

A <Term id="emergency-fund">buffer</Term> is one month of your own costs, kept where you can reach it. Not a figure from someone else's life: your room, your food, your travel, your phone.

Add up last month. Here it comes to ₹5,500.

| Last month | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Room share | ₹2,500 |
| Food | ₹1,800 |
| Travel | ₹600 |
| Phone and data | ₹300 |
| Course materials | ₹300 |
| One month of costs | ₹5,500 |

**Write it as a number and a date.** ₹5,500, and three payments of ₹2,000 pass it, so the date is your third payday.

A survey for the National Centre for Financial Education (NCFE) put a plain question to adults across India. Faced today with a major expense the size of one month's income, could you pay it without borrowing or asking family or friends? Across the country, 45% said yes.

## Growth on growth

Money left in an account can earn <Term id="interest">interest</Term>. The amount you put in is the <Term id="principal">principal</Term>.

Leave the interest in, and the next round of interest is worked out on the larger amount. That is <Term id="compound-growth">compound growth</Term>.

Compound interest was the hardest calculation in that survey: 6% got it right, and 11% of those aged 18 to 29.

Over 12 months it is a small effect. ₹2,000 a month at an illustrative 6% a year adds ₹671 to ₹24,000 of deposits. At 0% the same deposits stay ₹24,000.

**It is not ₹25,440, which is 6% on the whole ₹24,000.** Each deposit earns only for the months it has been there, and the last one has only just arrived.

## The same maths on what you owe

Interest compounds against a borrower the same way. A balance of ₹10,000 at 36% a year, charged monthly, is charged 3% of what is owed each month.

Repay nothing for 12 months and it is about ₹14,260. One flat 36% charge on ₹10,000 would be ₹3,600, so about ₹660 is interest charged on interest.

| ₹10,000 owed, nothing repaid | After 12 months |
| --- | --- |
| At 36% a year, charged monthly | about ₹14,260 |
| One flat charge of 36% | ₹13,600 |

Before you sign a retail term loan, a lender the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) regulates must hand you a <Term id="key-facts-statement">Key Facts Statement</Term>. It carries the <Term id="apr">APR</Term> and every charge, and a charge left off it cannot be added later without your consent. The rule covers those loans, not credit card balances.

**Read the total you repay against what you borrowed.**

## The rate is an assumption

Every rate in this lesson is a figure typed into a calculator, not an offer. Rates on accounts change, and the one quoted today is not owed to you next year.

Before comparing two rates, check what each one depends on, and whether the amount you put in can fall.

Prices move as well. That is <Term id="inflation">inflation</Term>, and a rough check is to take the rise in prices away from the growth rate.

**A buffer is not held in order to grow. It is held so the ₹4,500 repair does not become a loan.**

## Where a buffer can sit

Money you may need this week has to be reachable this week. This lesson does not pick a place for you, and the choice is yours to make.

One rule is worth knowing. In 2012 the RBI told banks to offer a Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account. It carries no minimum balance, and banks were told not to hold it apart from the accounts they offer everyone else. A small balance does not have to cost you a penalty.
