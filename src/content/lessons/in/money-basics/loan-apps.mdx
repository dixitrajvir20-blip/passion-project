## Who is behind the app

A loan app is only the front counter. The money comes from a lender: a bank, or an <Term id="nbfc">NBFC</Term>, a finance company registered with the Reserve Bank of India. **A lawful app names that lender before you agree to anything.**

Trap apps name nobody, or claim a tie to a real lender that they do not have. So check both names yourself. RBI publishes a list of digital lending apps and a list of registered NBFCs.

One limit: banks and NBFCs fill in the app list themselves. RBI does not check the entries, and a listing is not RBI approval. Still, if neither the app nor its lender is there, nothing shows an RBI-regulated lender behind it.

## What a lawful app never asks for

RBI's rules bar apps that lend for banks and NBFCs from reading your files and photos, your contacts or your call logs. They may use the camera, microphone or location once, for <Term id="kyc">KYC</Term>, and only with your consent.

**So an app that wants your contacts before it shows a rate has told you what it is.** RBI has publicly warned about unauthorised lending apps that dig into borrowers' phones and use rough tactics to collect.

On a shared phone, those contacts and photos belong to the whole family. Saying no and removing the app costs you nothing at this point.

## The full cost, in writing, first

Before you sign, a lawful lender gives you a <Term id="key-facts-statement">Key Facts Statement</Term>. It sets out the amount, each repayment, every charge and the <Term id="apr">APR</Term>: the yearly cost with <Term id="interest">interest</Term> and all fees added in. **A fee missing from the statement needs your clear agreement before it is charged.**

The money goes straight to your bank account, and you repay the lender directly. Then comes a <Term id="cooling-off-period">cooling-off period</Term> of at least a day. Within it you can hand the loan back with no penalty, paying the cost for the time you had it. A listed processing fee may be kept.

A trap app skips all of this. In this made-up case, ₹3,500 arrives and ₹5,000 is due in seven days.

## If an app is already threatening you

Some apps threaten to message your contacts, call your family, or raise the demand each day. **That is something to report, not a bill to pay in a panic.**

Report threats at cybercrime.gov.in, the national cybercrime portal, or to the police. If money was taken from you by fraud, call 1930 too. Keep screenshots, the app's name and every payment you made. Report the app on RBI's Sachet portal, sachet.rbi.org.in. Tell someone at home, and do not take a second app's loan to repay the first.

A lender on RBI's list has its own route. Complain to the lender first. If it rejects the complaint, or says nothing within 30 days, take it to RBI's complaint portal, cms.rbi.org.in.
