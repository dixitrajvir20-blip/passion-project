## Who is behind the app

A loan app is only the front counter. The money comes from a lender: a bank, or an <Term id="nbfc">NBFC</Term>, a finance company registered with the Reserve Bank of India. **A lawful app names that lender before you agree to anything.**

Trap apps name nobody, or borrow a real lender's name with no link to it. So check both names yourself. RBI keeps a list of digital lending apps on its website, and a list of registered NBFCs.

One limit: banks and NBFCs fill in the app list themselves, and RBI does not check each entry. It is a first check, not a guarantee. If neither the app nor its lender is there, nothing shows an RBI-regulated lender behind it.

## What a lawful app never asks for

RBI's rules bar apps that lend for banks and NBFCs from reading your files and photos, your contacts or your call logs. They may use the camera, microphone or location once, for <Term id="kyc">KYC</Term>, and only with your consent.

**So an app that wants your contacts before it shows a rate has told you what it is.** Apps outside the rules have used that kind of access to pressure borrowers. RBI has warned about their harsh ways of collecting.

On a shared phone, those contacts and photos belong to the whole family. Saying no and removing the app costs you nothing at this point.

## The full cost, on paper, first

Before you sign, a lawful lender gives you a <Term id="key-facts-statement">Key Facts Statement</Term>. It sets out the amount, each repayment, every charge and the <Term id="apr">APR</Term>: the yearly cost with <Term id="interest">interest</Term> and all fees added in. **A fee that is not on the statement cannot be charged later unless you agree to it.**

The loan lands in your own bank account, and you repay into the lender's account directly. Then comes a <Term id="cooling-off-period">cooling-off period</Term> of at least a day. You can hand the loan back with the cost for those days and no penalty, though a small processing fee may be kept.

A trap app skips all of this. ₹5,000 is approved, ₹3,500 arrives, and ₹5,000 is due in seven days.

## If an app is already threatening you

Threats to message your contacts, calls to your family, demands that grow each day: RBI has warned about exactly these ways of collecting. **They are something to report, not a bill to settle in a panic.**

Call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in. Keep screenshots of the messages, the app's name and every payment you made. Report the lender itself on RBI's Sachet portal, sachet.rbi.org.in. Tell someone at home, and do not take a second app's loan to repay the first.

A lender on RBI's list has a different route. Complain to the lender first. If it rejects the complaint, or says nothing within 30 days, take it to RBI's complaint portal, cms.rbi.org.in.
