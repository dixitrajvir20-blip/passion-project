## What a scheme is

A <Term id="government-scheme">government scheme</Term> is a programme with a written rulebook. It says who can join, what they get and how to apply. Who can join is its <Term id="eligibility">eligibility</Term>: an age, a family income, a class in school, a kind of work.

**The rulebook is published on an official government site, and that copy is the one that counts.** A forward, a video or a helpful stranger is at best a copy of a copy.

Schemes also come in kinds. Some pay money to you, like a scholarship. Some lend it, with help on the cost. Some are savings accounts a family pays into. A scheme that hands money to anyone who pays a fee fits none of these.

## A scholarship portal for students

The National Scholarship Portal is one place to apply for many government scholarships. Each student first makes a One Time Registration, tied to Aadhaar, which lasts through school and college.

For the 2026–27 round, students apply by 31 October 2026. The institute then verifies each application by 15 November. Payment goes by <Term id="direct-benefit-transfer">Direct Benefit Transfer</Term>, and the portal links to a page for tracking it.

**If a Common Service Centre, a local counter for online forms, does it for you, the portal fixes the charge for the whole job at ₹30.** A helper asking ₹1,500 for the same job wants fifty times that.

## A loan scheme for street vendors

PM SVANidhi lends <Term id="working-capital">working capital</Term> to street vendors: the fruit seller, the cobbler, the food cart. No collateral is needed, so nothing is pledged against the loan. Loans come in steps of ₹15,000, then ₹25,000, then ₹50,000.

**Repaying on time is built into the design.** It earns a 7% a year <Term id="interest">interest</Term> subsidy and opens the next, bigger loan.

A vendor holding a Certificate of Vending or a Letter of Recommendation applies on the scheme's portal or app, or with help from the municipal body. The official FAQ, dated 30 May 2026, says lending now runs until March 2030.

## A savings account for girls

Sukanya Samriddhi is not a payout. It is a savings account a guardian opens for a girl before she turns 10, at a post office or a listed bank branch. The family pays in between ₹250 and ₹1,50,000 a year, for up to 15 years.

**If one was opened for you, it becomes yours to run at 18.** From 18, or after Class 10 if that comes first, up to half of last year's closing balance can go on studies. The account ends 21 years after it was opened.

These rules are from an official note dated 21 January 2025. This lesson does not say whether any family should open one.

## Checking whether you qualify

Start from a site you open yourself, not from a link someone sent. myScheme is the government's search portal for schemes. You enter details like your age, family income and where you live, and it lists schemes you may fit.

**A match is a lead, not an approval.** Open the scheme's own page, read the rules and the last date, and apply where it says. Whether to apply is your call, or your family's.

Then look at how the address ends. Delhi Police's cyber cell says sites ending in .gov.in are genuine government sites. A look-alike ending in .in or .org deserves suspicion.

## How a fake scheme gives itself away

Fake sites borrow the names of real schemes, and messages send people to them. Delhi Police's cyber cell lists what they charge: registration fees, exam fees, document verification fees. A page dressed up to look official is <Term id="phishing">phishing</Term>.

The tells come in a bunch. A fee paid to a link or a UPI ID. Money for everyone, with no rules. A deadline of tonight. A request for an <Term id="otp">OTP</Term> or bank details. An order to forward it.
