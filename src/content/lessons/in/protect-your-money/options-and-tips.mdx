## What the study counted

SEBI's 2026 study of real trading accounts covers FY25 and FY26. Its trader-level figures come from the fifteen largest brokers, about nine in ten of all individual traders. They trade equity derivatives: contracts whose value follows a share or a share index.

In FY26, 89% of individual traders below 30 ended the year with a loss. Among traders above 60 the figure was 81%. Traders under 30 made up 43% of all individual traders, up from 31% in FY22.

**That 89% is a <Term id="base-rate">base rate</Term>: what happened to the whole group, before anything you believe about your own case.** Of these traders, 93% traded options only, and <Term id="index-option">index options</Term> carry almost all of that turnover.

## What the average works out to

<Term id="expected-value">Expected value</Term> is the average result once each outcome is weighted by how often it happens. A large gain that arrives rarely can still average out to a loss.

SEBI reports both sides of it. Among all individual traders in FY26, 88 in 100 lost money. The average loss among them was ₹1,47,000. The other 12 averaged a profit of ₹1,22,000.

**So the loss is about a fifth bigger than the gain, and it lands about seven times as often.** Across everyone who traded, the year averaged out to a loss of ₹1,17,000 per person.

## What nobody may sell you

Securities are investments that can be bought and sold, such as shares, bonds and fund units. SEBI's rules name two activities a person may not carry out on them without being registered or otherwise permitted. One is giving advice or a recommendation. The other is making any claim of returns or performance, stated or implied.

Brokers, exchanges, depositories and their agents must not associate with anyone doing either. A person engaged solely in investor education sits outside the rule, as long as they do neither.

**A channel that sells trade calls, and prints a monthly figure members can expect, is doing both.** The fee is not the problem. The advice and the claim are.

## Checking a registration takes a minute

SEBI keeps two registers on its own website: one for investment advisers, one for research analysts. Both are searched by name or by registration number. In September 2026 they held 1,045 and 2,221 entries. A <Term id="registered-adviser">registered adviser</Term> is on one of them.

**A screenshot, a number typed into a chat, or a badge on a profile is not a check.** The register is the check, and nothing said inside the group can stand in for it.

If the name is on neither register, nothing shows that the person may sell advice at all. That is worth knowing before ₹999 leaves your account, not after.

## If money has already gone

Call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in the same day if a fee was taken by fraud or an account was drained. Keep the transaction ID, the date, the amount and your screenshots.

SCORES, at scores.sebi.gov.in, takes complaints about SEBI-regulated entities: listed companies, registered intermediaries, and the exchanges and depositories. An unregistered tipster is none of those, so that route is closed.

Tell someone at home the same day. SEBI counted about 72 lakh individual traders in loss in FY26, so this is not a rare position to be in.
