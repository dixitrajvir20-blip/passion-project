## What the study counted

SEBI publishes a study of real trading accounts. The 2026 edition covers FY25 and FY26, and reads the profit and loss of every individual trader in the equity derivatives segment.

In FY26, 89% of individual traders below 30 ended the year with a loss. Among traders above 60 the figure was 81%. Traders under 30 made up 43% of all individual traders, up from 31% in FY22.

**That 89% is a <Term id="base-rate">base rate</Term>: what happened to the whole group, before anything you believe about your own case.** Of individual traders in the segment, 93% traded options only.

## What the average works out to

<Term id="expected-value">Expected value</Term> is the average result once each outcome is weighted by how often it happens. A large gain that arrives rarely can still average out to a loss.

SEBI reports both sides of it. Among all individual traders in FY26, 88 in 100 lost money. The average loss among them was ₹1,47,000. The other 12 averaged a profit of ₹1,22,000.

**So the loss is about a fifth bigger than the gain, and it lands about seven times as often.** Across everyone who traded, the year averaged out to a loss of ₹1,17,000 per person.

## What nobody may sell you

SEBI's rules name two activities a person may not carry out on securities without being registered or otherwise permitted. One is giving advice or a recommendation. The other is making any claim of returns or performance, stated or implied.

Brokers, exchanges and depositories must not associate with anyone doing either. Somebody engaged solely in education sits outside the rule, as long as they do neither.

**A channel that sells trade calls, and prints a monthly figure members can expect, is doing both.** The fee is not what makes it unlawful. The advice and the claim are.

## Checking a registration takes a minute

A <Term id="registered-adviser">registered adviser</Term> appears on SEBI's own register, at sebi.gov.in, searchable by name or registration number. The investment adviser list held about a thousand entries when this lesson was written.

**A screenshot, a number typed into a chat, or a badge on a profile is not a check.** The register is the check, and nothing in the group can stand in for it.

If the name is not there, the person is selling something they are not permitted to sell. That is worth knowing before ₹999 leaves your account, not after.

## When it is not trading at all

Some apps look like trading and are not. A screen asking you to stake money on where a price will stand in sixty seconds, for a fixed payout, is a bet on a number.

India's Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming law, passed in August 2025, bans <Term id="online-money-game">online money games</Term>, their advertising and the payments into them. A government backgrounder from October 2025 describes betting apps taking deposits on promises of large gains.

**An <Term id="index-option">index option</Term> is a regulated contract on a recognised exchange. A sixty-second stake is not.** Regulation describes what a thing is, not whether it suits you.

## If money has already gone

Call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in the same day if a fee was taken by fraud or an account was drained. Keep the transaction ID, the amount and your screenshots.

A complaint about a SEBI-registered intermediary goes to SCORES, at scores.sebi.gov.in. An unregistered tipster is not on any register, so there is nobody to complain to. That is the cost of paying one.

Tell someone at home the same day. This is common enough that the regulator measures it every year.
