## The PIN only sends money

Your <Term id="upi-pin">UPI PIN</Term> does one thing: it approves money leaving your account. **If a screen asks for your PIN so that money can reach you, the money is about to go the other way.**

Receiving needs nothing from you. Whoever is paying needs your UPI ID or your phone number, and that is all.

NPCI, which runs <Term id="upi">UPI</Term>, publishes the same rule: the PIN is needed for every payment, only sending money requires it, and it is shared with nobody. A <Term id="qr-code">QR code</Term> works the same way round. You share your own code to be paid, and you scan someone else's to pay.

## Every version wants the same approval

Most UPI fraud is one move: getting that approval out of you, or watching you give it.

The plainest version is a <Term id="collect-request">collect request</Term>. It asks you to pay, and the note beside it is typed by the sender, so it can say refund, advance or test.

The others change only the surface. A QR code sent with a promise attached. A helper app that shows a stranger your screen. A caller who wants a code. A uniform on a video call.

**Ask one question at every PIN page: am I trying to pay this person?**

## A call that wants a code

An <Term id="otp">OTP</Term> is a one-time code that confirms a single action. Read it out and you have approved that action for the caller. NPCI's safety points are short: never share an OTP, and enter your UPI PIN only on a UPI PIN page.

A <Term id="digital-arrest">digital arrest</Term> call is the same demand under more pressure: stay on camera, tell no one, move your money. India's cybercrime centre says Indian law has no such thing as a digital arrest, and that real officers do not arrest anyone over a video call.

**Hanging up costs nothing.** Then say what happened to someone at home.

## The first hour

**Call 1930 or report on cybercrime.gov.in before you do anything else.** Have ready your mobile number, the bank or app the money left from, the UPI ID or account it left from, the transaction ID, the date and a screenshot.

The helpline sends back an acknowledgement number. The official instructions say the complaint must then be completed on the portal within 24 hours. A police officer checks it and asks the bank holding the money to block it.

More than ₹11,158 crore was saved through that system in over 32.80 lakh complaints, counted to 30 June 2026.

## The three working days

RBI's rules on <Term id="customer-liability">customer liability</Term> cover payments you did not make yourself, and they turn on how fast you report. Where the fault lay neither with you nor with your bank, telling the bank within three working days of its alert leaves nothing on you. From the fourth working day to the seventh the amount is capped, and for an ordinary savings account that cap is ₹10,000.

Where you shared a PIN, a card detail or a code, the rules read differently. The loss up to the moment you report is yours, and what follows the report is the bank's.

**Report on the day you notice, working day or not.**
