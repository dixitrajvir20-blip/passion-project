## The PIN only sends money

Your <Term id="upi-pin">UPI PIN</Term> does one thing: it approves money leaving your account. **If a screen asks for your PIN so that money can reach you, the money is about to go the other way.**

Receiving needs nothing from you. Whoever is paying needs your UPI ID or your number, and that is all.

NPCI, which runs <Term id="upi">UPI</Term>, sets out the same rule. The PIN is required for every payment and is shared with no one. A <Term id="qr-code">QR code</Term> works the same way round: you share yours to be paid, and you scan someone else's to pay.

## One approval, many costumes

Most UPI fraud is one move: getting that approval out of you, or watching you give it.

The plainest version is the <Term id="collect-request">collect request</Term>, a bill dressed as a payment. The note beside it is typed by the sender, so it can say refund, advance or test.

The rest swap the prop. A QR code sent with a promise attached. A helper app that shows a stranger your screen. A caller who wants a code. A uniform on a video call. An app where the profit is only a display.

**Ask one question at every PIN page: am I trying to pay this person?**

## A call that wants money or a code

An <Term id="otp">OTP</Term> is a permission slip. Read it out and you have approved, for someone else, whatever the message describes. Banks do not ask for it, and the message says so in its own text.

A <Term id="digital-arrest">digital arrest</Term> call is the same demand under more pressure. It opens with a case you have never heard of. Then come the orders: stay on camera, tell no one, move the balance. No agency arrests anyone or collects money on a video call.

**Hanging up costs nothing and ends both calls.** Then say it out loud to someone at home.

## Money that exists only on a screen

The newer version arrives through a group chat. An app shows a balance, then a profit, then a charge to be paid before any withdrawal. The figures are drawn on a screen; only your deposits were ever real.

SEBI's investor material names the signals. An offer arrives that you did not ask for, with pressure to decide quickly. A high return is promised, and nobody sets out where the money goes. The same page says to check that the firm, and the people promoting a scheme, are regulated.

That check takes a minute on the regulator's own site. Pressure to decide quickly is what removes the minute.

## The first hour

**Call 1930 or report on cybercrime.gov.in before you do anything else.** Have ready your mobile number, the bank or app the money left from, the UPI ID or account it left from, the transaction ID, the date and a screenshot.

The helpline sends back an acknowledgement number. The official instructions say the complaint must then be completed on the portal within 24 hours. A police officer checks it and asks the bank holding the money to block it.

More than ₹11,158 crore was saved through that system in over 32.80 lakh complaints, counted to 30 June 2026.

## The three working days

RBI's rules on <Term id="customer-liability">customer liability</Term> depend on how fast you report. Where a breach was neither your fault nor the bank's, telling the bank within three working days leaves nothing on you. From the fourth working day to the seventh, the amount is capped, and for an ordinary savings account that cap is ₹10,000.

If you approved the payment yourself after being tricked, the rules read differently. The loss up to the moment you report is yours, and what happens after it is the bank's.

**So the day you report is the day the loss stops.** Report on the day you notice, working day or not.
