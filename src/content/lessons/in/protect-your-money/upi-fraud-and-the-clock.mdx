## The PIN only sends money

Your <Term id="pin">UPI PIN</Term> does one job on every <Term id="upi">UPI</Term> app: it approves money leaving your account. **If a screen wants your PIN so that money can reach you, the money is about to go the other way.** To be paid, you share only your number or your own code.

A <Term id="collect-request">collect request</Term> asks you to pay, whatever its note says. A <Term id="qr-code">payment code</Term> is scanned to pay and shown to be paid. A caller after your <Term id="otp">OTP</Term>, or a uniform on a <Term id="digital-arrest">digital arrest</Term> call, wants the same approval.

## The first hour and the three days

If money has gone, call 1930 or report on cybercrime.gov.in first. A police officer checks the report and asks the bank holding the money to block it.

Then tell your bank the same day. If you did not make the payment and shared no PIN or code, the Reserve Bank's rules on <Term id="customer-liability">customer liability</Term> leave nothing on you. That holds if you report within three working days of the bank's alert. These rules cover payments you did not approve. For one you approved after a lie, the fast report matters most.
