## What the ₹5,000 pays for

The work in the message is the whole job: money arrives, you send it on, you keep a cut. The money was taken from people who were defrauded, and your account stands between them and whoever took it. Someone whose account is used this way is a <Term id="money-mule">money mule</Term>.

An employer pays you from its own account, for hours you worked. This offer pays you out of other people's money, for the use of your name.

## Why the account stays yours

A bank opens an account only after a <Term id="kyc">KYC</Term> check on your identity documents. Every transfer in and out is recorded against that name, whoever types the instructions.

Banks report accounts they suspect into a shared <Term id="suspect-registry">Suspect Registry</Term>, and payments to them can be declined. Fraud money in a rented account can bring legal action against you, including arrest.
