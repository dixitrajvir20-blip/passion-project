## The pitch and the money

A <Term id="money-mule">money mule</Term> lets other people's money pass through a bank account. The pitch is a job: money arrives, you forward it, you keep a cut.

The money is usually taken from someone who has been defrauded. In October 2024 the national cybercrime centre described rented accounts being used to collect payments for fake investment sites and betting platforms.

**Forwarding is the work, and forwarding is the harm.** Money moved through an ordinary account, in an ordinary name, is money that is hard to follow.

No employer needs your account for money that is not its own. Pay for the use of an account is not wages.

## Your name is on every transfer

An account is opened with your <Term id="kyc">KYC</Term>: your ID, your photograph, your address. Every transfer through it carries that record, whoever typed the instruction.

A SIM works the same way. The connection is issued against the ID that bought it, and a card in someone else's hand does not move the name.

In September 2024 the Department of Telecommunications said it had disconnected over one crore mobile connections for involvement in cybercrime and financial frauds.

**Whoever collects the commission is the person the paperwork points to.** That is what is being bought.

## What the banks already do

Banks report the accounts they suspect, and those identifiers are shared through the <Term id="suspect-registry">Suspect Registry</Term> with the other entities taking part.

The government told the Rajya Sabha on 11 March 2026 that over 27 lakh mule accounts had been shared this way, and that transactions worth ₹9,518 crore had been declined.

Accounts caught up in these cases are frozen. In August 2026 the Supreme Court asked the Reserve Bank of India to write a standard procedure for mule accounts, and for frozen-account cases to be dealt with faster.

**The commission is the only money you see. The account stops working.**

| --- | --- | --- |
| Where your pay comes from | The employer's own account | The money you pass on |
| What is being used | Your hours | Your account and your ID |
| How it ends | You resign | A fraud complaint names you |

## The offers change, the ask does not

The same ask arrives in different wrappers. Money in, money out, for a commission. A month's rent for an account you barely use. Cash for a SIM bought in your name. A <Term id="task-scam">task app</Term> that shows earnings, then asks for a deposit before it pays.

**One question sorts them: am I being paid for work, or for the use of my name?** Hours, shifts and deliveries are work. An account, a card, a SIM and an ID are a name.

You do not need to know who is on the other end. The pitch itself is the tell.

## If you have already said yes

Move no more money. Tell your bank the same day, and report at cybercrime.gov.in or on 1930. Say plainly what you were offered and what you did.

Keep the messages, the account numbers, the dates and the amounts. Screenshots are enough.

The cybercrime centre's advice is to sell or rent nothing: not an account, not a business registration certificate. Money from fraud reaching such an account can bring legal action, including arrest.

**Reporting early is the part still in your hands.** An approach that never became a transfer is worth reporting too.
