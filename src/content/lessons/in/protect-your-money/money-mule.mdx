## The pitch and the money

A <Term id="money-mule">money mule</Term> lets other people's money pass through a bank account. The pitch is a job: money arrives, you forward it, you keep a cut.

The money is taken from people who have been defrauded. In October 2024 the national cybercrime centre described mule accounts being used to collect deposits for fake investment sites and betting websites.

**Forwarding the money is the work you are paid for.** Money that passes through an ordinary account, in an ordinary name, is harder to follow.

No employer needs your account for money that is not its own.

## Your name is on every transfer

An account is opened after a <Term id="kyc">KYC</Term> check on the identity documents you hand over. It stays in your name, whoever types the instruction.

A SIM works the same way. A connection is listed under the person whose ID took it, and a card in someone else's hand does not move that name.

In September 2024 the Department of Telecommunications said it had disconnected over one crore mobile connections for involvement in cybercrime and financial frauds.

**The commission is paid for the use of your name, not for hours of work.**

## What the banks already do

Banks report the accounts they suspect, and those details are shared through the <Term id="suspect-registry">Suspect Registry</Term> with the other institutions taking part.

The government told Parliament in March 2026 that over 27 lakh mule accounts had been shared this way, and that transactions worth ₹9,518 crore had been declined.

An account linked to cyber fraud can also be frozen. In August 2026 the Supreme Court asked the Reserve Bank of India to write a standard procedure for mule accounts. It also asked for cases about frozen accounts to be settled faster.

**Once an account is shared this way, the institutions taking part decline its transactions.**

| --- | --- | --- |
| Where your pay comes from | The employer's own account | The money you pass on |
| What is being used | Your hours | Your account and your ID |
| How it ends | You resign | Your account is named in a complaint |

## The forms the offer takes

The same ask arrives in different wrappers. Money in and money out for a commission. A month's rent for an account you barely use. Cash for a SIM bought in your name. A <Term id="task-scam">task app</Term> that shows earnings, then asks for a deposit before it pays.

The cybercrime portal's safety advice is to ignore an advertisement that promises money for little or no work.

**One question sorts them: am I paid for work, or for the use of my name?** Hours worked are the first. An account, a card, a SIM or an ID is the second.

## If you have already said yes

Move no more money. Report it on 1930 or at cybercrime.gov.in, and take the discrepancy in your account to your bank, which is part of the portal's own safety advice.

The reporting system was set up in 2021 for immediate reports, so that money can be stopped before it moves on.

A report can name the number, the handle, the page and the bank account you were given.

The cybercrime centre's advice is to sell or rent nothing: not an account, not a business registration certificate. Money from fraud landing in such an account can bring legal action, including arrest.

**An approach that never became a transfer is worth reporting too.**
