## Put every price on a year

<Term id="interest">Interest</Term> is the price of borrowing, quoted over different lengths of time. The card above charges 3.5% a month: 42% over a year.

An app taking ₹500 to lend ₹5,000 for seven days charges 10% a week. A year holds about 52 weeks, so that is about 521% a year. Divide the charge by the amount, then multiply by the periods in a year.

Before a digital loan, a lender regulated by the Reserve Bank must give you a <Term id="key-facts-statement">Key Facts Statement</Term>. Compare offers on its <Term id="apr">APR</Term>, the yearly cost with every charge included. Then a <Term id="cooling-off-period">cooling-off period</Term> of at least a day lets you hand the loan back.

## The minimum due is not the bill

Paying the <Term id="minimum-amount-due">minimum amount due</Term> counts as on time. But the card adds the month's interest to what you owe, and your payment covers that first.
