## What the 40% line measures

Your <Term id="net-pay">net pay</Term> is what reaches your account: €1,125 here. Eurostat, the EU's statistics office, sets housing costs against <Term id="disposable-income">disposable income</Term>: on one wage, about your net pay.

Housing costs are rent plus bills. Above 40% of income, Eurostat counts <Term id="housing-cost-overburden">housing cost overburden</Term>. On €1,125, 40% is €450; this room comes to €600, or 53%.

The line is a marker; the test is whether €525 covers food, travel, your phone and an <Term id="emergency-fund">emergency fund</Term>.

## What day one costs

A <Term id="rental-deposit">rental deposit</Term> of two months' rent is €1,040. With the first month, €1,560 is due when you collect the keys. Pay nothing before a viewing and a signed <Term id="rental-contract">rental contract</Term>. A price far below the market, a landlord abroad, money before keys: a known fraud pattern. A <Term id="european-consumer-centre">European Consumer Centre</Term> gives free advice when the business is in another EU country.
