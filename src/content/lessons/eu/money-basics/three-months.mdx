## The month that goes wrong

Almost half of EU adults aged 18 to 65 do not have enough saved to pay three months of living costs without borrowing. That is the European Commission's 2025 figure. Around one in six has nothing put aside for emergencies.

An <Term id="emergency-fund">emergency fund</Term>, or buffer, is money kept for the month that goes wrong. A laptop dies, a contract ends early, a room deposit falls due.

Without one, the surprise gets paid with debt: an <Term id="overdraft">overdraft</Term>, instalments at checkout, or a loan from a friend. Debt often costs <Term id="interest">interest</Term> or fees, and paying it back makes the next months start short. **That is why the buffer comes before anything else. It stops one bad month becoming a bad year.**

## Three months of what

The target is three months of needs, not three months of everything. Your <Term id="budget">budget</Term> already splits the month. Needs are what comes back whatever happens: your share of rent, food, a transport pass, your phone.

Take an apprentice in Linz on €950 a month. Needs come to about €650, so three months of cover is €1,950.

**The number is yours, and it moves with you.** Rent differs a lot between cities and countries, so the same three months is a different sum for everyone. Some EU countries, such as Poland and Sweden, have their own currency. There, you work the target out in that currency.

## Slowly is the plan

A target of €1,950 can feel out of reach on €80 a month. So build it in steps, and count each step as done when you reach it.

The first step is one surprise: about €300 in this example. Then one month of needs. Then three.

**The habit matters more than the rate.** Move a fixed amount on payday, before the first spend, so saving is not whatever is left over. At a low rate, nearly all of the buffer is money you put in yourself.

## Where it waits

Two tests decide where a buffer waits. You can reach it within a day or two, and its value cannot fall. That is <Term id="liquidity">liquidity</Term>. Keeping it apart from your spending money makes it harder to spend by accident.

A low rate may not keep up with <Term id="inflation">inflation</Term>. For this money, that is the price of knowing it will be there. Where exactly it waits is your choice, and this lesson does not pick one for you.

## What the guarantee covers

In every EU country, money in a bank account is covered by a <Term id="deposit-guarantee">deposit guarantee</Term>. If the bank fails, the scheme pays it back, up to €100,000 per person, per bank, or the same amount in local currency.

Company shares, <Term id="investment-fund">investment funds</Term> and crypto are not bank deposits. Their value can fall just when you need the money, and the deposit guarantee does not cover that. If the place holding your money is not a bank, ask how the money is protected.
