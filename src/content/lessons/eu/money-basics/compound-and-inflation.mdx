## Why growth speeds up

<Term id="interest">Interest</Term> is worked out on the balance. Put in €1,000 at 2% a year, and the first year adds €20. That €1,000 is the <Term id="principal">principal</Term>.

In year two the balance is €1,020, so 2% adds €20.40. The extra 40 cents is interest on last year's interest. That is <Term id="compound-growth">compound growth</Term>. It starts small and widens every year the money stays.

## What inflation takes back

Prices rise as well. That is <Term id="inflation">inflation</Term>, measured across the EU by the <Term id="hicp">HICP</Term>. The euro's <Term id="central-bank">central bank</Term> aims for inflation of 2% a year.

A balance is a <Term id="nominal-value">nominal value</Term>: euros counted. Its <Term id="real-value">real value</Term> is what they buy. If prices rise 2% while your €1,000 grows to €1,020, it buys what €1,000 bought a year ago.

Take the rate, subtract inflation, and read the sign: below zero, your savings buy less.
