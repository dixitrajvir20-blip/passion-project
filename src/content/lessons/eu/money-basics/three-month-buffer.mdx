## The goal, and the date

An <Term id="emergency-fund">emergency fund</Term>, or buffer, pays the month when income stops. Without one, that month goes on an <Term id="overdraft">overdraft</Term> or loan.

Size it from essentials, not <Term id="net-pay">net pay</Term>: the rent share, transport, phone and food that still arrive with no pay. Three months of those is the goal: €730 × 3 = €2,190, not €3,450.

Divide the goal by the months you give it: €2,190 ÷ 12 is about €185, moved on payday before spending decides what is left. <Term id="interest">Interest</Term> adds little.

## Where it waits

It must be reachable within a day or two, which is <Term id="liquidity">liquidity</Term>, and steady in value.

In every EU country, a <Term id="deposit-guarantee">deposit guarantee</Term> covers bank accounts. If the bank fails, a national scheme repays up to €100,000 per person, per bank. <Term id="investment-fund">Investment funds</Term> are not deposits. Their value can fall, and no guarantee covers that.
