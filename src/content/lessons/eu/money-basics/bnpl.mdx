## Four payments is a loan

At checkout, the button says four payments of €30 and no interest. The shop is still paid today. A lender pays it, and you pay the lender back. That is <Term id="buy-now-pay-later">buy now, pay later</Term>.

**Once the first €30 is paid, you owe €90. That is a debt, even with no <Term id="interest">interest</Term> on it.**

The price stays the same. Splitting it changes when you pay, not how much. What the plan adds is three dates you must be ready for.

## New EU rules from 20 November 2026

Until now, the EU's consumer credit rules left out loans under €200 and loans with no interest or charges. Many pay-later plans fell through that gap. The revised Consumer Credit Directive brings most of them inside the rules from 20 November 2026.

**One change you will notice is a check before the loan.** A lender must look at your income, your spending and what you already owe. That is a <Term id="creditworthiness-check">creditworthiness check</Term>, and from that date pay-later plans get one too.

Each EU country writes the directive into its own law, so details such as limits on charges can differ. For the rule where you live, ask your country's consumer authority.

## Three small plans, one big bite

One plan of €30 looks small. The trouble starts when plans stack. Say you earn €600 a month from a weekend job. Three plans are running, and this month each one takes two payments: €60, €40 and €50.

**Plan payments are fixed, like rent. They leave on their dates, whatever else the month brings.** What shrinks is the money left for wants and savings.

So before adding a plan, add up what the running plans take this month. That total, not the new payment, is the number to hold against your <Term id="budget">budget</Term>.

## When a payment will not go through

A 0% plan costs nothing only while every payment lands on time. If your pay arrives after a due date, the payment can fail and a <Term id="late-fee">late fee</Term> is added; the plan's terms say how much. The timing of money in and out is your <Term id="cash-flow">cash flow</Term>, and here it sets the cost.

**Act before the date, not after.** Contact the lender, move money if you can, and ask what can change. Borrowing to cover a payment, say with an <Term id="overdraft">overdraft</Term>, only moves the debt.

If several plans are slipping at once, you are not the first. The revised directive requires every EU country to offer independent debt advice at little cost. Ask your consumer authority where to find it.
