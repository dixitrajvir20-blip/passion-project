## What one sale abroad costs

Take a single poster. Sold in Portugal at €25, it costs €6 to print, €1.50 to pack and €4 to post. The sale keeps €13.50. That is its <Term id="margin">margin</Term>.

Send the same poster to Austria and the printing and packing still cost €7.50. The postage changes: €11, against €4 at home. Postage is a <Term id="variable-cost">variable cost</Term>, paid again on every sale.

**Hold the €25 price with delivery included, and the extra €7 comes out of the €13.50.** Only the cost of delivering it changed.

## The 14-day right is part of the cost

Buyers who order online from an EU business, for themselves, have a <Term id="right-of-withdrawal">right of withdrawal</Term>. For goods it runs 14 days from delivery, no reason needed.

If a buyer cancels, you refund the price and the standard delivery charge within 14 days of being told. Extra paid for faster delivery stays with you.

The buyer pays return postage only if your page said so before the order. Say nothing and that cost is yours. Leave the right off the page and the buyer gets a further year to cancel.

Goods made to order for one buyer, or personalised, sit outside the right. **Some orders come back. The postage already paid on them is still a cost.**

## Carrying the postage, or charging it

There are two common ways to cover the postage. Put the postage inside the price and offer delivery included, or show the price and add postage by country.

Either way the buyer has to see the total, with delivery and any other charge, before placing the order. The same page says who pays if the poster comes back.

One price with delivery included is simpler to show. It works only when it covers the most expensive country you post to. **Build it on the €4 you pay at home, and every order abroad takes €7 out of what the sale keeps.**

## Whose VAT applies above €10,000

<Term id="vat">VAT</Term> is a tax on sales. The buyer pays it inside the price, and a seller who charges it passes that part to the tax authority. So the part you keep is the price without the VAT.

One EU-wide limit governs <Term id="distance-sale">distance sales</Term>: €10,000 a year in total to buyers in other EU countries. Under it, you can charge your own country's rate. Over it, the rate of the buyer's country applies.

Charging the buyer's rate could mean registering in each of those countries. The <Term id="one-stop-shop">One Stop Shop</Term> exists so one registration and one return cover them all. **Whether a small seller registers at all is set nationally, so take that question to your own tax authority.**

What the sale keeps goes towards the month's <Term id="fixed-cost">fixed costs</Term> before any of it is profit. If that poster comes back and the return postage is yours, the month carries another €11. In the EU countries that do not use the euro, the same sums run in the national currency. **Price a cross-border sale from what that sale costs, not from the price at home.**
