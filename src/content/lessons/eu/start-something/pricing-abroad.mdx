## One bag, two trips

Look at a single bag. At home in Portugal it sells for €20. The blank bag and the ink cost €6, the packaging €1 and the post €2. You keep €11. That €11 is your <Term id="margin">margin</Term>.

Now send the same bag to Austria. The bag and the ink cost the same. The post does not: in this example it costs €7, not €2. Postage is a <Term id="variable-cost">variable cost</Term>, because every sale pays it again.

**Keep the €20 price with free delivery, and the extra €5 comes straight out of the €11.** The bag is the same. The sale is not.

## Returns belong in the price

Online buyers in the EU have a <Term id="right-of-withdrawal">right of withdrawal</Term> when they buy from a business. For goods, they have 14 days from delivery to change their mind, with no reason needed.

If they cancel, you refund the price and the standard delivery charge, within 14 days of being told. They pay the return post only if your shop said so before they ordered. Leave out the cancellation details and they get up to a year longer.

Goods made to order or personalised, and food that spoils quickly, are not covered.

**Some bags will come back, and the post you paid to send them is gone.** A little set aside from every sale, €1 here, covers them.

## VAT: part of the price may not be yours

<Term id="vat">VAT</Term> is a tax on sales. The buyer pays it inside the price, and a seller who charges it passes that part to the tax authority. **If you charge VAT, work out what you keep from the price without it.**

Each EU country sets its own VAT rates, within shared EU rules. The standard rate cannot be lower than 15%.

Many small sellers do not charge VAT at all. Each country sets a yearly sales limit under which a small business can apply to be exempt. No country's limit is above €85,000.

## Whose VAT, when you sell abroad

For a seller who does charge VAT, the buyer's country starts to matter. While online sales to buyers in other EU countries stay under €10,000 a year in total, your own country's VAT applies. Above that, the buyer's country's rate applies. One registration at home can cover the sales to every EU country.

So one price can hold a different amount of tax in Austria, Ireland and Belgium. **The rules on who charges what are national, so ask your own tax authority before you start.** A friend's answer from another country is not yours.

## One price for many countries

You can show one price with delivery included, or a price plus postage by country. Either way, the buyer must see the total, with every extra charge, before placing the order.

One price with free delivery is simpler to show. It works when it covers the most expensive country you post to. Here is €25 for one bag sent to Austria.
