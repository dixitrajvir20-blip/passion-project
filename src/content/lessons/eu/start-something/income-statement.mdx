## Top line, bottom line

An <Term id="income-statement">income statement</Term> covers one stretch of time, here March. It starts from <Term id="revenue">revenue</Term>, what the work sold for, counted when you send the <Term id="invoice">invoice</Term>. Any <Term id="vat">VAT</Term> you charge stays off it: you collect that for the tax authority.

Revenue minus <Term id="cost-of-sales">cost of sales</Term>, what the jobs used, is <Term id="gross-profit">gross profit</Term>. Take off the month's running costs, its <Term id="operating-expenses">operating expenses</Term>, and <Term id="net-profit">net profit</Term> remains. Only that bottom line says whether the month paid.

## Profit is not cash

The software and the desk are <Term id="fixed-cost">fixed costs</Term>: €490 is owed even in a month with no work. Travel is not; it stops when the jobs do.

A March invoice paid in April still counts in March, so the account can hold less than the profit. The timing of money in and out is <Term id="cash-flow">cash flow</Term>.
