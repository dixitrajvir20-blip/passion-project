## What the platform sends

Since 2023, an online platform in the EU must collect its sellers' details and report them once a year. The rule is <Term id="platform-reporting">platform reporting</Term>, or DAC7. It covers personal services, sales of goods, and renting out property or transport.

The report names you, your address and your tax number. It gives what you were paid each quarter, how many jobs that covered, and the fees the platform charged.

**A report filed in one EU country still reaches the tax authority where you live.** The Dutch tax administration passes on the reports it receives. In Ireland, Revenue says the platform must also give you a copy by 31 January.

## What a return asks for

The €400 is what learners paid through the platform. It is not what the work left you.

A <Term id="platform-fee">platform fee</Term> of €60 never reached you. Travel to lessons and printed worksheets cost another €90. Both are <Term id="business-expense">business expenses</Term>: costs of doing the work you were paid for.

Finland's tax administration puts it on the seller to report both the income received through a platform and the expenses that go with it.

**Take the €60 and the €90 off, and €250 is left: the <Term id="net-profit">profit</Term> the return is built on.** The platform's report shows the €400 and its own €60. It cannot show your €90.

## Thresholds are set by each country

Taxes on personal income are the business of each member state, so there is no single EU figure for small earnings. The same sums run in national currency outside the euro area.

In Ireland, Revenue sets the line at €5,000 of taxable income that payroll has not taxed, or €30,000 before costs. Above either, you register for <Term id="self-assessment">self-assessment</Term>. Below both, Revenue points to a Form 12. **A threshold decides which form you file, not whether the income counts.**

<Term id="vat">VAT</Term> has its own limit: no higher than €85,000 a year, and lower where a country sets it lower. Whether you register as a <Term id="sole-trader">sole trader</Term> is a third question. Your tax authority and your country's <Term id="point-of-single-contact">point of single contact</Term> answer them.

## Keep a record as you go

The hard part of a return is rarely the arithmetic. It is working out, in March, what a bus fare in October cost.

<Term id="bookkeeping">Bookkeeping</Term> here means one dated line per payment and per cost, with the receipt kept behind it. That record is what lets you take the €90 off.

Finland's tax administration is direct about it: “You continue to have responsibility for including all the amounts you have received in your tax return”. Its page adds that platform data is not automatically added to a pre-completed return.

**So a <Term id="tax-return">tax return</Term> is still yours to fill in.** The Dutch tax administration makes the same point: the figures it receives do not set your tax bill by themselves.
