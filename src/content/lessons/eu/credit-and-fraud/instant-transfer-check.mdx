## What the name check tells you

An <Term id="instant-credit-transfer">instant transfer</Term> lands within ten seconds and cannot be taken back. The money follows the <Term id="iban">IBAN</Term>, whatever name you type beside it. So before you send, your bank runs a name check, called <Term id="verification-of-payee">verification of payee</Term>, with the bank behind that IBAN.

A match means the names agree. A close match shows the real name, for you to compare. No match means the account is not in the name you typed. The bank warns you, but the choice stays yours.

The check cannot tell you whether a message is true. Check a changed account by a route you had before it arrived, never by the reply button.

## Why the check comes before the button

A card payment you never made is an <Term id="unauthorised-payment">unauthorised payment</Term>. Your bank refunds it, and in most cases no more than €50 falls on you. A transfer you approved, even after a lie, is an <Term id="authorised-push-payment">authorised push payment</Term>. **The refund rule does not cover it, because the bank did what you asked.** The account you paid often belongs to a <Term id="money-mule">money mule</Term>, who moves the money on quickly. So if money has gone, call your bank at once.
