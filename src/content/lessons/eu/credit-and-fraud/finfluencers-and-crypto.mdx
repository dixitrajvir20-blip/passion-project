## Who gains when you tap

A <Term id="referral-link">referral link</Term> or code, a gift or a free account is payment. A post that carries one is <Term id="paid-promotion">paid promotion</Term>, whatever else it is. <Term id="esma">ESMA</Term>, the EU's markets regulator, asks creators to label it in plain words, such as Ad or Sponsored.

A creator who already holds what they praise has a <Term id="conflict-of-interest">conflict of interest</Term>, and that belongs in the post too. A "not financial advice" line changes neither. **The first question about any money post is who gains when you act on it.**

## Check the firm, then the grey line

Offering investment services in an EU country takes a national regulator's permission. A firm that has it is an <Term id="authorised-firm">authorised firm</Term>, listed on that regulator's register. Firms that trade or hold <Term id="crypto-asset">crypto-assets</Term> for you need permission too. Crypto can lose everything, so a promised monthly gain is a warning sign.

Adverts for <Term id="contract-for-difference">contracts for difference</Term> carry a <Term id="risk-warning">risk warning</Term>: the share of that firm's own <Term id="retail-client">retail</Term> accounts that lose money. It describes people in your position. A fixed sum or nothing on a price guess is a <Term id="binary-option">binary option</Term>, which firms may not sell to retail customers.
