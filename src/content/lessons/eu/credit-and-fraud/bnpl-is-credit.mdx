## The lender pays the shop today

At checkout, the button offers four payments of €30 at 0% interest. The shop is still paid in full today. A lender pays it, and you pay the lender back. That is <Term id="buy-now-pay-later">buy now, pay later</Term>, and it is a loan.

**After the first €30, you still owe €90 on that one purchase.** Zero <Term id="interest">interest</Term> says what the debt costs. It does not say there is no debt.

The price does not move. Splitting it changes when you pay, not how much. What the plan adds is three dates your account has to be ready for.

## Three plans, one date

One plan of €30 looks small next to a month. Three plans across two lenders is <Term id="loan-stacking">loan stacking</Term>, and their payments land together.

Say three purchases are running: trainers at €120, a winter coat at €144 and course books at €96. Each was split into four. Next month each takes one instalment.

**Between them they take €90, all on the 1st, the day the rent leaves.**

| Purchase | Price | Instalment due on the 1st |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trainers | €120 | €30 |
| Winter coat | €144 | €36 |
| Course books | €96 | €24 |
| All three | €360 | €90 |

## What the month has left

Net pay is what lands in the account after deductions. Here it is €1,200 a month. Rent and bills take €540. Food, transport and phone take €264. That leaves €396, and the three instalments take €90 of it.

**Instalment dates are fixed, like rent. They leave on their day, whatever else the month brings.** What shrinks is the part you still choose.

So the figure to hold against your <Term id="budget">budget</Term> is not the new €30. It is every instalment due that month, added up.

## What changes on 20 November 2026

EU consumer credit rules used to leave out credit under €200, and pay-later plans sat outside them too. The revised rules cover both.

Those rules are the Consumer Credit Directive, an EU law that each country writes into its own. They apply from 20 November 2026.

Three parts of it you can use. A <Term id="creditworthiness-check">creditworthiness check</Term> before the credit is granted. Clear information about the credit before you agree. And 14 days to cancel the agreement, without giving a reason.

Because each country writes its own version, limits on charges differ. Ask your national consumer authority for the rule where you live.

## When a payment does not land

A 0% plan stays free only while every payment lands on time. If pay arrives after a due date, the payment can fail and a <Term id="late-fee">late fee</Term> is added. The plan's terms set the amount.

Take €15 as an example fee on the €120 trainers: an eighth of the price, on a plan sold as interest-free.

In the Netherlands, pay-later providers charged a late fee about 1.8 million times in 2024, across 53 million transactions, the AFM reported in July 2025. That is about one in 30.

**Act before the date, not after.** Contact the provider and ask what can change. Paying it from an <Term id="overdraft">overdraft</Term> moves the debt and adds charges of its own.

## Before you add another plan

One habit covers most of this. Before tapping a new plan, list every instalment already due that month, add the new one, and hold the total against what the month has left.

A <Term id="credit-limit">credit limit</Term> will not do that for you. It is the most the lender is willing to lend, not what your month can carry.

If several plans are slipping at once, that is what <Term id="debt-advice">debt advice</Term> is for. Under the same rules, every EU country must set up debt advice services. Your national consumer authority can say where.
