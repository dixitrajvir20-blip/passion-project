## What the €200 is paying for

The advert calls it an internship, but the only work is receiving transfers and sending them on. The money has usually been taken from someone. Europol links more than 90% of the mule transactions it finds to cybercrime, such as <Term id="phishing">phishing</Term>.

Your 5% is the price of your account standing between the victim and the thief. Someone who lets an account be used this way is a <Term id="money-mule">money mule</Term>.

## Why not knowing may not protect you

Moving money that came from crime is <Term id="money-laundering">money laundering</Term>. EU law requires member states to make it a crime that can carry prison. It also lets them charge someone who ought to have known where the money came from. The European Banking Federation warns that a mule may never again open a bank account or get a mortgage.

A real employer pays you from its own account, for work you did, and the money stops with you. It needs your <Term id="iban">IBAN</Term>, never your card or its <Term id="card-security-code">security code</Term>.
